# BURNED Origin Trailer — Requirements

## Problem Frame

UMB has an origin trailer (Trailer V3, ~124s, shipped to `projects/undercover-mob-boss/videos/trailer/out/`). BURNED does not. The asymmetry matters: BURNED is the more visually ambitious, more architecturally interesting project, and the engineering-peer audience that watched UMB's autonomous-SDLC trace deserves a BURNED equivalent that *raises the bar* — not just clears it.

The bar-raise is the point of this trailer. UMB v3 used solo noir narration and a chronological SDLC trace. BURNED has access to a richer toolkit: seven named operative characters in the roster (Dash, Vera, Sable, Janet, Neal, Otto, Agent X) plus Dolores Grieves as a recurring NPC, Archer-the-show visual vocabulary, an in-world Pendleton Agency setting with diegetic briefing-room props (dossiers, comms ticker, venetian blinds), a locked product spec, and a sustained visual identity. The trailer should land each of these as instruments, not just decoration.

Audience is engineering peers / agentic-SDLC showcase — same lane as UMB v3. The trailer should make engineers want to talk about how it was built, not just want to play it.

**The bar-raise is the repeat itself, not new engineering substance.** UMB v3 told the autonomous-SDLC story for the first time; BURNED's trailer is the second proof point. The engineering claim isn't "we found new SDLC content to surface" — it's *"UMB wasn't a lucky shot."* Two autonomous-SDLC products shipped at Archer-grade is the repeatability proof that matters more to engineering peers than any single new artifact would. The richer toolkit (characters, Pendleton vocabulary, briefing-room props) is how we make the repeat *land*; the repeat itself is what the trailer is selling.

## Requirements

**Narrative Frame**

- **R1.** Spine: in-world Pendleton Agency briefing. The trailer presents itself as a real briefing on "Operation Pendleton" — the operation of building BURNED itself. No external narrator; the briefer is a Pendleton character speaking to the agency. **Note on R14:** the cold-open hook precedes the briefing's diegetic frame and is also in-world (different operative, casual debrief register) — it does not violate R1. R1 governs the briefing scene specifically, not the entire trailer.
- **R2.** Tone: deadpan, played straight. Comedy comes from the *gap* between in-world spy vocabulary and SDLC reality. The briefer never breaks frame, never winks at the camera, never acknowledges that the "operation" was actually a software build.
- **R3.** Climax: visual + audio stacked. A receipts cascade carries the visual payoff while a Dash deadpan VO reveals that *"the autonomous field assets, the forensic dossiers, the mission rehearsal artifacts — they weren't preparing for the operation. They WERE the operation."* The cascade then dissolves into live gameplay footage as the closer.
- **R14.** **Cold-open hook — compressed Archer-title-sequence shape + repeatability declaration.** The trailer opens with a **compressed title-card cold-open inspired by the iconic Archer opening title sequence** — bold operative cards flashing with names + roles, chevron / target-reticle motifs, mid-century geometric typography (Saul Bass / Pablo Ferro lineage), brass-jazz hook. Channels the structure; does NOT copy specific FX/Floyd County IP. **3–4 quick operative card flashes** (cold-open speaker's card foregrounded + 1–2 others establishing cast density), bold BURNED logo treatment, the casual debrief line dropped over the music hook. A different operative (TBD: Sable, Vera, or Janet) speaks the line in casual register, shape: *"Yeah, so, the kid did it AGAIN. Honestly at this point I'm just impressed."* The line declares the repeatability proof in the first 5 seconds — the trailer's highest-attention real estate. Double-meaning gag: "he's a machine" reads as colloquial admiration to a naive viewer AND literally as "the machine did it" to an engineering audience. R14 works in concert with R15's on-screen text signals to telegraph the agentic-SDLC origin. **Duration cap: ≤8 seconds** — compressed from Archer's typical ~30s title sequence; we land 3–4 cards instead of 6–8. **Line shape requirement:** the chosen cold-open line MUST contain the "machine" double-meaning hook (or equivalent wordplay loading "autonomous" / "wrote itself" / "built itself"). Candidate lines that only convey "they shipped another one" without the machine wordplay fail to carry R14's stated load. The cold-open speaker is the trailer's third voice (or second if Vera doubles duty here AND on the scream). **Fallback:** if R14 falls back to non-voice cold-open (no character voice authenticates per R5-research-gate), the title-card structure REMAINS — operative card flashes + brass hook + BURNED logo + agentic-SDLC stamp from R15 — only the spoken line is dropped. The title-sequence vocabulary stands alone.
- **R15.** **On-screen text signal layer — agentic-SDLC origin, diegetic.** R14's audio hook is one mechanism; R15 is the parallel VISUAL mechanism. At least two in-world but unambiguous text signals telegraphing the autonomous-SDLC origin must appear on-screen across the trailer's runtime, in Archer-coded chrome typography. Examples (planning will pick specific copy): classification stamp **"OPERATION PENDLETON / CASE FILE 02 / METHOD: AUTONOMOUS"** landing in the cold-open; comms ticker line **"OPERATIVE [REDACTED] — METHOD REPEATABLE"**; dossier stamp **"AUTONOMOUS FIELD UNIT — ASSET DELIVERED"**; title-card subhead **"AGENT-BUILT, ARCHER-GRADE"** at the closing dissolve. The signals remain in-world (R6 still holds — no raw SDLC vocab) but are visually explicit enough that an engineering peer with NO context can read them as "this is autonomously built" without relying on R14's wordplay alone. **Acceptance:** at least one signal lands in the cold-open frame, at least one in the cascade or closer. The R14-decode-gate tests the audio mechanism; the success criterion (a)+(b) is now carried by R14 + R15 together, not R14 alone.

**Voice & Performance**

- **R4.** Primary briefer: **Dash** (Sterling-cadence). Owns ~90% of the runtime. Voice must match Dash's already-established character DNA across cards/help-file/dramatic-beats text — not a new "Dash voice" invented for the trailer.
- **R5.** Cameo: **Vera**. Punctuates the briefing. Must include at least one authentic "**VERA!!!**" scream in the iconic Sterling-screams-Lana cadence. *If the scream cannot be made Archer-grade authentic, it gets cut — flat scream worse than no scream.* (See Outstanding Questions, R5-research.) **Cut-handling:** if R5 is cut, Vera is removed entirely from the trailer — no non-scream substitute cameo, no consolation line. **Resolution order with R14:** R5 authenticity is evaluated FIRST. If R5 is cut, Vera is also removed from R14's cold-open candidate set — the cold-open speaker is re-selected from {Sable, Janet}, or R14 falls back to the non-voice cold-open per R5-research. R14 is not a backdoor to a non-scream Vera appearance. The beat sheet is restructured to a Dash-solo runtime, and whichever scene was structurally built around the scream collapses to a Dash deadpan punctuation. Vera fails *closed*, not open.
- **R6.** Script must stay in Pendleton in-world vocabulary end-to-end. Translation key: *agents → autonomous field assets / spec, autopsy → forensic dossiers / tests → mission rehearsal artifacts / code → operational tradecraft / deploy → field deployment / commits → log entries.* No raw SDLC term reaches the audio.

**Format & Production**

- **R7.** Runtime: **90–100 seconds**, structured as 5–7 scenes.
- **R8.** Aspect ratio: **16:9 landscape only**. No 9:16 vertical cut, no short-form cutdown.
- **R9.** Music bed mood: **Archer-coded mid-century brass / bossa** — bold horns, syncopated rhythm, faint vibraphone. Source (generative / royalty-free / licensed) deferred to planning.

**Receipts Cascade Content**

- **R10.** **How-to-play dossier** — the 10-act in-game help file (`src/client/howtoplay/`, acts: mission, cover, roster, arsenal, loop, intercept, combos, turn-inheritance, remote, signoff). Hero receipt; gets its own beat in the cascade.
- **R11.** **Goofy stats overlays** — quantitative receipts written for **comedy first, impressiveness second**. The Archer-tone corrective. Generic "167 tests passing" type stats are LinkedIn-coded and tonally wrong on their own; pair every dry stat with an absurd one. Specific stat list developed in planning.
- **R12.** **Imagen-generated card art** — selected illustrations from the **17 unique Imagen-generated artworks** at `public/assets/cards/` (covering all 120 cards in the deck via per-type duplication, plus Agent X as the wild card). Visual proof the Archer-grade bar was hit at the asset level. Selection is curation across 17 files, not sampling across 120.
- **R13.** **Live gameplay footage closer** — actual phone-controller + TV-shared-screen multiplayer capture. The briefing dissolves into reality here. BURNED is shipped (1407 tests, clean build, no live prescriptions per `TODO.md` verified 2026-05-15) — gameplay is capture-ready today.

## Success Criteria

- Trailer passes the §2 Quality Bar in `docs/PRODUCT-SPECIFICATION.md` — *"Could this look like a frame from an Archer episode?"* — applied to every shot, transition, and overlay.
- The water-beads-off-it standard: the magic of agentic-SDLC autonomy disappears, the joy of the product takes over.
- An engineering-peer viewer, with no setup, recognizes both *(a)* the agentic-SDLC origin story — telegraphed by R14's audio hook + R15's on-screen text signal layer working in concert — and *(b)* that BURNED is a real playable game — by the closing gameplay dissolve. R6 is preserved (no raw SDLC vocabulary in audio); the telegraph runs through diegetic Pendleton chrome (stamps, comms ticker, dossiers) that an engineering peer parses as the agentic-SDLC origin without breaking the in-world frame.
- The VERA scream is Archer-grade authentic OR it's cut. Shipping a flat scream is worse than not having one.
- The receipts cascade lands as "this team has discipline + this team has TASTE" — not as "this team has a lot of artifacts."

**Tiebreaker rule.** When (a) "engineers talk about how it was built" and (b) "water-beads / product-joy takes over" conflict in the edit bay, **water-beads wins**. The build is the subtext; the game is the text. The cascade earns its place by feeling like Archer set-dressing, not a credits roll.

**Bar-raise criteria (relative to UMB v3).** The trailer must clear UMB v3 on at least one of these dimensions on a fixed sampling protocol — 10 frames sampled at fixed timecodes (every ~10s) from each trailer:

1. **Named-operative density** — average count of named Pendleton operatives visible per sampled frame (silhouette, portrait, dossier photo, illustration panel). Measures the "richer cast toolkit" claim from Problem Frame.
2. **§2 frame-pass rate** — across the 10 sampled frames, how many would independently pass §2 Quality Bar ("could be a frame from an Archer episode") on a fixed yes/no rubric (composition, palette discipline, typographic discipline). Threshold: ≥8 of 10. Sampling is what makes this adjudicable — not per-frame across 2700 frames.
3. **Stacked-payoff moment** — does the trailer have a single beat where visual + audio reveal land simultaneously (cascade + Dash VO reveal per R3)? UMB v3 doesn't have this. Binary yes/no.

Parity-with-UMB is not the goal; relative advance on at least one of these axes is. **Axis 4 from the prior version (absence of LinkedIn-coded stats) dropped** — R11's cold-read-through gate already enforces this; it's not a bar-raise dimension, it's table stakes.

## Scope Boundaries

- **No vertical / 9:16 cut.** Engineering audience watches on desktop/landscape; one banger > two compromises.
- **One paper dossier hero per cascade beat** — *principle, not prohibition*. The how-to-play dossier is the current candidate. Spec, convention files, plans, and ADRs are NOT in the cascade. The autopsy is a credible fallback alternative for the hero slot if how-to-play under-delivers visually in capture (planning-phase call). The rule that holds is one paper hero, not a specific paper hero.
- **No multi-operative ensemble.** Voice cast is **2–3 operatives**: Dash (required, ~90% runtime), cold-open speaker (required — Sable, Vera, or Janet), Vera scream cameo (conditional on R5 authenticity). Maximum three distinct voices; Vera-doubling (cold-open AND scream) reduces to two. Otto, Neal, Agent X, Dolores do not voice-cameo. No voice beyond this set regardless of TTS-authenticity outcomes. **Gameplay capture audio is music-bed-only** — any player voice in the captured clip must be stripped or never captured; the voice cap of 3 includes the captured clip's audio surface.
- **No live-action footage** of Briggsy or any human.
- **No wink-at-camera tone breaks**, no fourth-wall acknowledgment of the audience.
- **No runtime above 100s or below 90s.** Force economy.
- **No SDLC vocabulary in the audio.** All translation through R6.

## Key Decisions

- **Audience = engineering peers** drives every other choice. Same lane as UMB v3, raised bar.
- **Bar-raise = repeatability proof, not new engineering substance.** The engineering claim this trailer makes is *"the autonomous-SDLC method works twice"* — UMB v3 wasn't a lucky shot. Production polish (Archer-faithfulness, Pendleton briefing format, multi-voice, dossier-grade artifact storytelling) is how we make the repeat *land*; the repeat itself is the engineering substance. Considered and explicitly declined: adding autopsy / convention files / playtest harness to the cascade to chase "new engineering content." That framing inverted the priority — the *re-execution* of the method is more compelling to engineering peers than any single new artifact would be. Document-review product-lens reviewer flagged this 2026-05-15; position defended.
- **Cold-open hook signals agentic-SDLC origin to no-context viewers.** R6 forbids SDLC vocabulary in the briefing audio — that creates a marketing failure mode where a viewer who hasn't seen UMB v3 can't decode the agentic-SDLC origin in real time. Fix: R14 — the trailer opens with an in-world casual-debrief line (*"the kid did it AGAIN..."*) before the formal briefing begins. Double-meaning gag — colloquial admiration AND literal "the machine did it" — telegraphs the agentic-SDLC origin in the first 5 seconds without breaking R2 (the cold-open precedes the briefing's diegetic frame, doesn't break it from inside). Document-review product-lens reviewer flagged the failure mode 2026-05-15; R14 closes it.
- **Case-file briefing spine** chosen over autobiographical narrator because it lands Archer vocabulary natively in a way UMB couldn't access.
- **Played-straight tone** chosen over hybrid wink for maximum rewatchability and Archer-faithfulness. One bad wink dies the bit.
- **Dash as briefer** chosen over Janet/Otto/Dolores because Sterling-cadence briefings *are* the show's archetype, AND because Dash's voice is the most established in BURNED's existing copy.
- **90–100s runtime** chosen over 124s (UMB v3 reference: 9 scenes / 124s ≈ 13.8s/scene). At 5–7 scenes, BURNED's scene density is *comparable to UMB*, not categorically denser — the "shorter and denser" framing was rhetorical, not mathematical. The actual bet: bar-raise comes from per-frame quality (every shot Archer-grade) and a stacked-payoff climax UMB v3 doesn't have, not from runtime arithmetic. See Success Criteria "Bar-raise criteria" for the explicit evaluation axes.
- **16:9 landscape only** chosen over dual-format to focus production on one polished output.
- **Stacked climax** (visual cascade + audio reveal in same beat) chosen over single-channel payoff because the two reinforce each other; engineering-twitter remembers stacked moments.
- **Gameplay-footage closer** chosen over autopsy-closer because the cascade is already artifact-centric and the gameplay reveal provides the "show, don't tell" punctuation that pure artifacts can't deliver.

## Outstanding Questions

### Resolve Before Planning

- **[R4-acceptance-gate][Affects R4][P0]** **Dash TTS authenticity gate.** Before the planning phase commits to beat-sheet structure, the chosen TTS engine (default: UMB's Gemini pipeline) must produce a 15-second Dash sample script. **Sample-script discipline:** the test paragraph MUST be drawn from or stylistically verified against existing Dash copy (cards, HTP dossier, Phrasing! pool, DramaOverlay text) — not invented in isolation, which would test TTS engine capability rather than Dash-character fidelity. **Tester protocol:** ≥2 Archer-fan testers, neither of whom is Briggsy and neither of whom has read the BURNED card text.
