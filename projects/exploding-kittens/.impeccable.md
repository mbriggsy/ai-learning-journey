# Exploding Kittens Digital — Design Context

## Users

Friends and family in the same room. One TV/laptop as the shared screen (landscape), each player on their phone (portrait). Party game night energy — people are talking, laughing, heckling. The board is the stage; the phone is your secret hand.

**Context matters:** The TV is across the room (3m readability). Phones are in one hand, beer in the other. Touch targets are fat. Attention is split between screen, phone, and the humans yelling at you.

**The job:** Play Exploding Kittens with friends, digitally, with zero setup friction and maximum drama. The game is the excuse to be in the room together — the presentation makes it memorable.

## Brand Personality

**Playful. Jaw-dropping. Overpolished.**

This is a silly game about exploding cats presented with cinematic production value. The gap between subject matter (absurd) and presentation (premium) IS the personality. Think Pixar: silly premise, world-class execution.

The quality bar is Undercover Mob Boss — everyone who plays it talks about the graphics, the theme, how it all ties together. EK doesn't have to be dark like UMB, but it must match or exceed that cohesion and polish. "So fucking slick water beads off it."

## Emotional Arc

Chill (lobby/waiting) → Curious (whose turn?) → Tense (drawing a card) → EXPLOSIVE (boom/defuse/nope chain) → **Laughing.**

The destination is always laughing. The drama, the tension, the theatrical eruptions — they're all setups for the comedic punchline. This is not a serious strategy game. It's an excuse to scream at your friends while a cat explodes on a TV.

## Aesthetic Direction

**"Neo-Noir Casino"** — controlled darkness with theatrical eruptions of color.

- **Board (TV):** Dark noir surfaces (`#0c0a12` → `#12121f` → `#1a1a2e`). Overhead spotlight feel. Near-monochromatic default state. Color ERUPTS during actions and fades after. Only 4 card types get neon accents; everything else is muted warm slate.
- **Phone:** Warmer, lighter surfaces. Adapts to OS light/dark preference. Phone needs to pop in ambient light (lit room, not a dark theatre). Light mode uses warm parchment, not sterile white.
- **Cards:** Premium feel. Subtle accent-tinted gradient, glow on hover/select, 5:7 aspect ratio. Each cat type has its own personality color. Card backs have crosshatch pattern with inner border frame.
- **Typography:** Clash Display for headlines/card names (bold, uppercase, tracked). General Sans for body/descriptions. JetBrains Mono for numbers/counts (tabular-nums).
- **Motion:** Three tiers — Ambient (subliminal breathing/pulses), Action (card plays, draws), Theatrical (explosions, defuses, game over). Framer Motion with LazyMotion strict mode (`m` not `motion`).

### References

- **Undercover Mob Boss** — the quality and cohesion bar. Everyone talks about the production value. Polish level is non-negotiable.

### Anti-References

- **Cyberpunk** — oversaturated neon-on-dark is dead. EK uses restraint; color means something when it appears.
- **Luxury/refined** — too quiet for a party game. This needs ENERGY.
- **Maximalist** — fights readability at 3m on a TV. The void matters.
- **Generic mobile game** — player phones must NOT feel like a cheap remote control. First-class experience.
- **Jackbox cartoon style** — EK has its own visual language. Premium, not illustrated.

## Accessibility

- **Color blindness:** Primary user is color blind (assume worst-case red-green deficiency). Claude owns ALL color decisions. IBM CVD-safe player colors. Nope is teal, not green. Each cat type differentiated by unique hue.
- **Redundant signals:** Color NEVER carries meaning alone. Always paired with shape, icon, label, position, or animation.
- **Reduced motion:** Full `prefers-reduced-motion` support. Every animation has a non-motion fallback.
- **High contrast:** `prefers-contrast: more` and `forced-colors: active` handled in CSS.
- **Focus management:** Two-ring focus indicators (visible on dark backgrounds). Keyboard navigation throughout.
- **Touch targets:** Minimum 48x48px. Phone UI designed for one-handed use.

## Design Principles

1. **Polish is the product.** "Too much polish isn't enough polish." Every surface, every transition, every microinteraction gets the water beads treatment. If it's "fine," it's not done.

2. **Drama serves comedy.** The cinematic lighting, the theatrical card reveals, the tension-building Nope windows — they're all setups for the punchline. The emotional destination is always laughing.

3. **Phone is a first-class citizen.** The player's phone experience needs as much design love as the TV board. It's not a remote control — it's half the game. This is the hardest design challenge and the biggest gap today.

4. **Color tells the story, shape tells the truth.** Color creates drama and personality, but accessibility demands that meaning is always redundant. Shape, icon, label, and position carry the facts. Color is the emotion.

5. **Restraint makes eruptions matter.** Near-monochromatic default state. When color explodes, it means something. One neon at full intensity at a time. The quiet is the setup; the drama is the punchline.
