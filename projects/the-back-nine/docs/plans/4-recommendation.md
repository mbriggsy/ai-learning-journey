## The eight cross-cutting contracts

*These eight contracts span the four units below. They are named once here so a reader sees the seams, then enforced in the unit that owns each — mirroring the contract sets that govern the earlier acts. Several are seams the earlier acts explicitly handed forward into an Act-4 home — the validated spine + CRN draw schedule (Act 1), the tax-and-accounts + healthcare overlays (Act 1 / Act 3), the `memoryModel` orchestrator + request-epoch (Act 2), the `require-the-hedge` lint (Act 3), and the per-surface staleness map (Act 3); this section is that home.*

1. **Validation gates the solver — U14 is built and PASSING before U15 is allowed to recommend (owned cross-cutting by Units 14→15; the recommendation half of the SC two-tier correctness contract).** The success criteria are two-tier: the engine's *number* is right (Trinity/Bengen golden cases, Act 1) **and** the solver's *recommendation* is right (the "Solver validation" strand of [docs/research/engine-validation-and-tax.md](../research/engine-validation-and-tax.md)). Unit 14 is the second tier and it **gates** Unit 15: the **optimality/ranking oracle** (hand-computable known-best drawdown/conversion cases), **ranking-stability under CRN**, and **grade calibration** must each exist and pass before the solver may emit a recommendation to any surface. This is the discipline that makes a confidently-wrong recommendation **fail loud**, the way Trinity/Bengen make "the number is right" testable. Until the oracle passes, any solver output is **directional** (marked so in code) and no Unit-16 surface may render it as a recommendation. **The order is structural, not a convention — and structural means *type-level*, not a boolean:** the "oracle-cleared" result is an **opaque nominal token** constructable **only** by the validation harness on a clean pass, and Unit 15's `solve`-as-recommendation entry point takes that token as a **required parameter** — so shipping a recommendation without a passing oracle is a TypeScript *compile* error, never a discipline failure (mirroring Act 1's no-unkeyed-write seam, where no write path *exists* without a key). The gate's own test plants a wrong-best case the oracle must reject (`burned/070`). **`fixtures-pinned` is itself a checked clause of the token, not an assumption:** the harness mints the cleared token **only when every rec-relevant §Strand-5 + healthcare primary the near-ties depend on is pinned (`directionalUntilPinned === false`) *and* ε is calibrated** (contract #2) — a test plants a single still-directional rec-relevant constant (or an uncalibrated-ε sentinel) and asserts the token is **refused** and the solver refuses to recommend, so the gate can never go green on the directional fixtures that decide the very near-ties the optimizer overfits.

2. **The held-out-seed discipline — select on A, grade on B (owned by Unit 14; consumed by Units 15+16).** Argmax over many candidates on a single seed overfits *that seed's* noise (the optimizer's curse), so the in-sample winner's score is optimistically biased. The defense: **select** the winning strategy on **seed-set A**, then **report its graded confidence on an independent held-out seed-set B**. **Both seed-sets are themselves CRN-correct** — within A, all K candidates share the one-draw-per-year schedule (contract #5); within B, the same. A and B must be **statistically independent, not merely "distinct"**: `seedA` is the existing Act-2-minted scenario seed (so the spine headline still reproduces under it — Act 1), and `seedB` is derived by a **hash / SplitMix expansion of `seedA` into a well-separated stream** — never `seedA + 1` or a near-integer, whose mulberry32 streams correlate and silently make B optimistic. This is seed-*input* decorrelation only; it does **not** replace mulberry32 as the path PRNG. **Only `seedA` is persisted** — it is the scenario's existing Act-2 seed field, surviving the encrypt→decrypt round-trip bit-identically; `seedB` is never written to disk, because `deriveSeedB` is canonical and `isCanonicalSeedB` structurally refuses any other value (`heldOutSeed.ts:48/67`), so re-deriving it on re-entry is byte-exact and a persisted copy could only ever disagree with it. The **survival-equivalent ε has two distinct roles that must not be conflated:** the **display band** (the rendered uncertainty interval) is measured on **B**; the **selection tie-tolerance** that declares two candidates survival-equivalent is a *selection* decision and must **never** be a B-measured level band — using B to choose re-contaminates the held-out. The selection tolerance is a pre-specified / theoretical standard error (or computed A-side), keyed to the variance of the pairwise **difference** between candidates under CRN (which CRN shrinks far below the variance of the level — a level-keyed band over-declares ties and can drop a survival-relevant gap down into Tier-2). Deciding the near-ties on the selection seed's noise *is* the curse. Compounded landmine: directional-until-pinned fixtures decide the exact near-ties the optimizer overfits, so **pinning the §Strand-5 + healthcare primaries is a hard solver PREREQUISITE**, not a residual gate.

3. **Deterministic, byte-identical recommendation selection (owned by Unit 15; the recommendation analogue of Act 1's seed/headline identity).** The selected strategy + its grade + its runner-up must be **byte-identical across reproductions and across re-entry** — a recommendation that jitters between runs cannot be trusted with money. Selection = **a quantized objective** (the lexicographic score rounded to a stable display/decision granularity so float noise can't reorder near-ties) + **a deterministic lexicographic tie-break** (a fixed candidate ordering: policy enum order, then conversion amount, then years) + **a seeded sub-stream ORTHOGONAL to the simulation draws** (a third stream, never one of seed-sets A/B, used only where a tie-break needs a deterministic coin — never for simulation, so it cannot perturb any draw schedule). No `Math.random`, no clock, no `getRandomValues` inside the solver (the engine-purity lint of Act 1 already forbids them in `src/engine/**`). The selection is reproducible from `(model, seedA, seedB, goal)` alone. **Byte-identical is a same-JS-engine guarantee** (Act 1 scopes the cross-engine reproduction claim and owns the quantized-headline guard that makes the *displayed* `X of 10` robust to a last-ULP transcendental difference across browser engines): the selection run and the held-out-B grade for one recommendation must execute on the **same engine instance**, never a precomputed cross-engine baseline, or a single ULP delta in one `Math.exp` could reorder a near-tie and silently bias the grade.

4. **Objective ≡ headline metric, lexicographic (owned by Unit 15 for the objective; Unit 16 for the render).** The objective is **lexicographic** (R21): **Tier 1 (the floor)** = the survival-floor statistic — essentials covered every year across futures, expressed in the spine's **`X of 10`** natural-frequency metric; **Tier 2 (the user picks)** = a surplus goal, **each a concrete distributional statistic the headline renders directly** (there is no separate internal score the headline could contradict). **Two shipped, and they are the whole live vocabulary** (`RECOMMENDATION_GOALS`, `model.ts:270` — the engine's objective, the codec's `needVocab` gate and the intake `GoalPicker` all read that one array): **_pay less tax_** = lifetime tax paid (real dollars); **_leave more_** = the bequest measured **after-tax to heirs** — the gross estate adjusted by a *first-order* §1014 / IRD treatment (taxable basis stepped up at death, inherited pre-tax taxed as ordinary income-in-respect-of-a-decedent at a **disclosed assumed heir bracket**, Roth tax-free), **never gross / pre-heir-tax** (a gross objective can crown the strategy that leaves *less* after-tax — contract #7). A third, **_live bigger now_** = the held-out **`X of 10` confidence that the user's chosen front-loaded (go-go-years) discretionary spending shape holds across futures**, was specified here and **deferred past U15**: it ranks a confidence statistic rather than a distributional dollar, so its objective wiring is its own increment and it joins the vocabulary additively. Whenever it lands the standing rule is that it is a confidence-over-*fixed*-spending statistic, **never a spend-amount the solver raises** (the budget stays read-only, contract #8a; telling the user how much *more* they could sustainably spend is a continuous-spending / die-with-zero solver, explicitly chapter-two). **The quantity the solver maximizes IS the quantity the headline shows** — Tier-1 score ≡ the survival headline, and when survival is a given the headline pivots (contract #6) to the **same** Tier-2 quantity the solver optimized — so a recommendation can never recommend a move that worsens its own hero number. Candidates within the **selection tie-tolerance** (the CRN-difference-keyed tolerance of contract #2, computed at selection — never the B-measured *display* band) are **survival-equivalent**; among them the winner is the best Tier-2 score. Seed-set B carries the displayed number + the grade, **never** the choice of which candidates are survival-equivalent. **Spending shape is never an objective term** (contract #8) — the solver optimizes *funding*, not how you live.

5. **The single shared market draw per year — now MORE load-bearing (owned by Unit 15; one-line extension of Act 1's CRN contract + Act 3's draw contract).** All account buckets (pre-tax / Roth / taxable / **HSA**) share **one** market-return draw per year; buckets differ only in tax treatment, never in return assumption. This is what lets the solver rank **K candidate strategies on identical futures** (signal, not luck) and it **structurally forecloses asset-location**. Act 3 proved this for a 2-arm Roth what-if; **Unit 15 generalizes the CRN test to K candidates** — every policy × conversion-grid candidate, within a seed-set, consumes the normals **identical path-for-path across the survivor MFJ→single transition**. Per-bucket draws are forbidden (they break CRN and re-open asset-location). The death-order **conditional filter** carries through unchanged: same seed, same draws, no re-draw.

6. **The recommend-second flow + the 10/10→surplus headline pivot (owned by Unit 16; consumes the Act-2 spine answer + the Act-3 budget two-tier reading).** The spine confidence answer is the **first** beat (the magic moment, unchanged — never overwritten). The recommendation is the immediate **second** beat — *"and here's what we'd do about it"* — with the comparative reasoning (why this beat the runner-up) **one tap down** (depth-on-demand, R4). The user **tunes / overrides / re-picks the goal**, both futures updating. It is **calm and invited**, never a nagging alert/badge/engagement bait (R11). **The 10/10 clamp pivot:** when survival is a given (over-funded household / Tier-1 already maxed across futures), the headline honestly **switches** to the chosen surplus metric — the delta itself is the hero, *"this keeps ~$X more from the IRS"* — so the solver's signal is never eaten by the survival clamp. The active metric is always rendered explicitly so the user never silently re-bases between the spine beat and the recommendation beat. **The pivot never says "you're safe either way"**: that is an absolute survival-ceiling claim in the 70–90% band where `docs/decisions/market-model.md` §3–§6 names survival overstated, it was VETOED by the 2026-07-18 honesty hawk (ruling 7 above), and `copyGuard` now bans the phrase in both its contracted and uncontracted forms (`copyGuard.ts:182-185`).

7. **The hedge-on-headline lint + the disclosed-omission rule (owned by Unit 16; consumes the Act-3 `require-the-hedge` lint + the Act-2 copyGuard catalog).** Every recommendation **grades its own confidence** and **the hedge rides the headline** — depth-on-demand must never invert into certainty-on-top / caveats-hidden-below (R22). copyGuard's **`require-the-hedge`** lint (a *positive/require* assertion introduced in Act 3, not the legacy ban-list) is extended to assert the recommendation headline + the grade copy each wear their probabilistic hedge by construction; certainty verbs stay banned (R12). All recommendation strings route through the `src/ui/copy.ts` catalog (the recommendation viz in `src/viz` stays string-free, receiving text as props — `burned/063`). **Disclosed-omission rule:** because a disclosed omission can **invert a ranking** (not just blunt a delta), ACA-PTC + IRMAA are already **IN** the model (Act 1). **State income tax is IN and priced** for the {NC, PA, FL} roster (ruling 1 above) and is out-but-disclosed only for a household outside it; **NIIT** is the one lever-inert effect that is out-but-disclosed everywhere. Whatever is out is disclosed in plain language **adjacent to the delta**, never silently dropped. One further effect is **lever-*sensitive*** and so is handled separately from the lever-inert NIIT/state pair: when the chosen Tier-2 goal is *leave more*, a death-time **§1014 basis step-up** (taxable stepped up at death, inherited pre-tax fully taxable to heirs as IRD, Roth tax-free) **moves with the sequencing/conversion lever** and can invert which strategy leaves more *after-tax* to heirs. By the IN/OUT line's **own falsifiable test** (the lever moves which account is preserved into the estate, *and* the effect can invert the ranking) it therefore belongs **IN**, not disclosed-and-omitted — a disclosure cannot fix a ranking the solver argmaxed on the wrong quantity (gross), which is the calm-but-wrong recommendation shape this whole act exists to forbid. So the *leave more* objective is the **after-tax-to-heirs** bequest (contract #4): a **first-order** §1014 / IRD adjustment is modeled into the bequest statistic, with the **assumed heir tax bracket** carried as a *disclosed, R7-editable assumption* (surfaced adjacent to the leave-more delta) rather than the unmodeled gross figure. Lever-**inert** NIIT (and state tax outside the priced roster) remains the OUT-but-disclosed residue — neither moves which account is preserved; the full-fidelity estate model — multiple heirs, state estate tax, trusts, the precise IRD timing — stays chapter-two.

8. **Spending is never solver-recommended (owned by Unit 15) + the saved-rec re-entry gate (owned by Unit 17).** **(a)** The solver's controls are **only** the two funding levers — named drawdown policies × the conversion grid; the **budget / spending shape is user-set input** (the floor essentials + the discretionary surplus the funding is optimized for), never a control the solver moves. A tool must never recommend a *lifestyle*; it shows honest consequences. **(b)** A saved recommendation is a **real action a household may be executing**, so it may be spoken in the present tense only when the build can still stand behind it. **The drafting premise here was that this INVERTED the spine's rule — it does not, and the refutation is ruling 6 above: the spine never re-presents under a saved vintage either; it always recomputes under current rules, and the persisted seed is what makes the recompute byte-identical when no clock has fired (`staleness.ts:17-21`).** What actually distinguishes a recommendation is the *severity of the failure*, and the shipped rule is a **three-conjunct re-entry gate** (`src/store/savedRecommendation.ts`): the remembered verdict is current iff the fresh solver-run fingerprint matches the record's, **and** `SOLVER_CODE_VERSION` matches, **and** no rulebook clock fired **against the record's own era snapshot** — never against the scenario's stamps, which `scenarioFromDraft` re-mints at every save and which would therefore read "no clock fired" over a ranking priced under superseded law. Fail any conjunct and the surface shows the record as **superseded**, naming **every** cause that holds (`inputs-changed` · `inputs-unavailable` · `solver-changed` · `rules-changed`), plus an **invited** re-open that names its cost. **Never an automatic re-solve** — the solve was measured at 45.9× a single simulate, so silently spending minutes on re-entry is not a calm behavior — and never a remembered ranking re-drawn as if it were today's. Re-verifying the **ACA legislative status** rides the `rules-changed` conjunct through the healthcare vintage (it can flip the whole pre-65 model and thus which strategy wins). A saved *manual-control* result (an Act-3 what-if, not a committed action) carries no such record and stays on the plain staleness path.

**Approach.**

- **The optimality oracle = hand-computable known-best cases.** The fixtures are cases where the best drawdown/conversion order is *known by independent derivation*, not by the engine's own formula (a golden the engine computes via its own path proves typing, not correctness — see the externally-derived-fixture discipline in [docs/architecture.md §5](../architecture.md)). Each is re-derived at test time from the canonical constants by closed form. Five worlds shipped, plus two state companions of (i) described after them:
  - **(i)** the conventional **taxable → tax-deferred → Roth** drawdown ordering is optimal **only in a constant-marginal-rate world** — a flat rate, or income high enough that every year sits in one bracket, with **no RMD, no SS taxation, no cap-gains stacking, no ACA/IRMAA cliff, and no bequest motive** — these stated as the fixture's gating preconditions. (A *low-other-income* world is explicitly **not** this case: it is the bracket-filling regime where (ii), not conventional ordering, is the hand-derived best — so the oracle must never be hardcoded to conventional ordering there.)
  - **(ii)** a **bracket-filling Roth conversion optimum** — fill to the top of a target bracket and no further — in a **stripped** case where **SS taxation, cap-gains/QD stacking, and all cliffs are OFF**, so the in-bracket marginal rate is genuinely flat and "bracket top" actually equals "today's marginal = future marginal" (the real rule is marginal-rate equalization; "bracket top" is a coincidence of the simplified world, never the rule the solver should learn).
  - **(iii)** a **cliff-aware case** where filling past an ACA 400%-FPL ceiling or an IRMAA step **loses**, so the healthcare-blind ranking is sign-inverted (a healthcare-blind delta makes aggressive conversion look better than it is — the shipped fixture builds that counterfactual from the engine's own premium surface, and it crowns the over-cliff loser) — here the binding ceiling is the subsidy-aware MAGI / IRMAA-step line, **not** the bracket top, so (ii) and (iii) are **separate worlds** (healthcare off vs on) and a fixture's known-best is never applied across that boundary.
  - **(iv)** a **leave-more after-tax case** where the **gross**-bequest-best strategy is *not* the **after-tax**-best — the §1014 step-up / IRD treatment (contract #4 / #7) inverts the ranking (e.g. preserving a taxable bucket that steps up at death beats converting a pre-tax bucket the heirs would owe IRD on at the assumed heir bracket) — so the oracle's known-best is the **after-tax-to-heirs** winner and a solver that argmaxes the gross bequest **fails loud**; this is the hand-derived guard that the after-tax objective is the one actually optimized.
  - **(v)** a **no-change case** where the conventional drawdown + zero conversion *is* the known-best for the `(model, goal)`: the oracle asserts the solver routes to the **no-change state** (Unit 16) and does **not** crown a fabricated active move — which requires the search space to **always** contain the conventional-order / conversion-0 candidate as a labeled baseline (Unit 15).
  - **The two state companions of (i)** (ruling 1): an **NC** twin, where the state term flips the optimal conversion anchor from the 22% top to the 12% top — the concrete demonstration of why the state precondition refusal is load-bearing rather than bookkeeping — and a **PA** twin that is byte-identical to the state-absent original, since a PA conversion at ≥59.5 costs $0.
  - The oracle asserts the solver's **ranking** puts the known-best candidate first; a **planted wrong-best** fixture makes the oracle **fail loud** (`burned/070`), with a control arm proving the assertion is not vacuous.
- **Ranking-stability under CRN, generalized to K candidates (extends Act-3's 2-arm test).** The *ranking* — not just each pairwise delta — must be stable on the shared draw matrix. Assert all K policy × conversion-grid candidates, within one seed-set, consume the normals **identical path-for-path across the survivor MFJ→single transition**; perturbing only a candidate's conversion amount must not perturb any other candidate's draw consumption. **Pair every CRN absence-assertion with a presence companion** (`burned/027`): assert "the harness ran N paths and at least one path crossed the survivor transition / an ACA cliff / an IRMAA step" so a vacuous zero-path pass cannot masquerade as stability.
- **Grade calibration — "just do it" really is robust, "coin-flip" really is a coin-flip.** The grades are **robust-across-futures ("just do it")** vs **fragile ("coin-flip — here's what it hinges on")**. Calibration asserts: a "just do it" grade is emitted **only** when the winner's lexicographic advantage over the runner-up holds on the **held-out seed-set B** with margin **beyond the ε-band**; "coin-flip" when the held-out advantage falls **within** noise. The oracle hand-supplies one **known-robust** case (a dominant strategy that wins on any futures) and one **known-fragile** case (two near-tied strategies) and asserts the grade matches. **The N=1 cold-read judges the grade's *tone*; this oracle is the only judge of its *correctness*** (R25) — they are different gates and neither substitutes for the other. Because a **single** held-out B is unbiased but **high-variance**, the grade is computed over a specified **minimum B path floor** (enough paths that the margin-vs-tolerance decision is not itself noise) and a **grade-stability check** asserts the grade for a fixed `(model, goal)` does not flip across independent held-out-B draws — a known-borderline case is forced to the conservative ("coin-flip") reading rather than allowed to luck-flip. **The "independent held-out-B draws" are a deterministic B-*family*, not plural persisted seeds:** the one persisted `seedB` indexes a fixed, well-separated SplitMix/hash family `seedB[0..m-1]` of `m` independent hold-out streams (`m` = `solverBFamilySize` — a distinct quantity from `solverMinBPaths`, the path floor each stream must clear; both values live in `constants/solver.ts`), all **re-derivable from the single `seedB`** — so the grade is both stability-checked *and* **byte-reproducible on re-entry** (Unit 17 re-solves on the saved `seedB` and regenerates the identical family → the identical grade, never a grade that can't be reproduced). The per-solve compute profile (Unit 15) must budget the `m`-draw family, not one B. **The grade also emits its "what it hinges on" named driver** (consumed by Unit 16, contract #7): `gradeCalibration.ts` runs a small **sensitivity probe** — re-rank the top candidates under the ACA-enhanced-subsidy toggle and a fixture-vintage perturbation — and reports the single input whose flip moves the grade (e.g. "depends on whether enhanced ACA subsidies return"); a pure seed-B near-tie with no identifiable input driver carries a **`sampling-noise-near-tie` sentinel**, never a fabricated input cause. **Grade vs. display resolution are reconciled:** a graded-significant advantage that rounds **below one display tenth** of the `X of 10` headline must not render an *unchanged* `X of 10` under a "just do it" (indistinguishable from no-change) — the surface either shows the finer figure (the surplus `$` delta / a percentile-shift line) or the grade requires the advantage to clear **both** the seed-B ε-band **and** one display tenth before "just do it" (the sub-tenth win otherwise collapses into the no-change state). Seed-set B and the grade thresholds are drawn under a **fresh-draw, used-once-per-release, re-drawn-on-any-change-to-candidates-or-thresholds** protocol; the N=1 cold-read may revise tone/copy but its feedback **never** re-tunes the candidate set, thresholds, or the survival-equivalence tolerance against B-derived outputs (adaptive reuse silently re-optimisms B — the garden-of-forking-paths failure).
- **The held-out-seed defense + the ε-band measurement (contract #2).** `heldOutSeed.ts` runs the candidate evaluation on **seed-set A** (selection) and **seed-set B** (reporting), each CRN-correct internally. **Seed-set B carries the *displayed* numbers, not only the grade:** the recommended figure and the no-action baseline Unit 16 renders both come from B (the `seedA` selection score is never displayed — else the hero number is the curse-biased one). The K-candidate ranking-stability/CRN check (above) therefore runs on **both** A and B, since B now carries the rendered delta as well as the grade + the ε-band. The **survival-equivalent ε** — the Tier-1 tolerance within which candidates are "equivalent" and the Tier-2 goal decides — is **calibrated here against the optimality oracle** (the constant is this unit's deliverable). ε is stored with an **out-of-range sentinel default** (−1), never an in-range fallback (`burned/062`): an unset/uncalibrated ε is *detectably* unset, never a plausible-looking 0.0x that silently overfits, and the token refuses with `epsilon-uncalibrated` rather than throwing at mint time. The selection half was **pre-specified 2026-07-18, before any held-out-B draw existed** (`solverSelectionTieZ` = 1.96, the two-sided 95% normal quantile — deliberately not the date-search's one-sided 1.645, which answers a different question), which is what keeps the fresh-draw protocol below honest. **`seedA` is the existing Act-2-minted seed; `seedB` is derived by a hash/SplitMix-expansion of `seedA` into a well-separated stream (never `seedA + 1`)** so B is a provably *independent* hold-out, not a near-copy whose mulberry32 stream correlates with A — a seed-input decorrelation only, not a mulberry32 replacement. **The ε resolves into two calibrated quantities (contract #2):** a **display band** measured on B (the rendered interval) and a **selection tie-tolerance** keyed to the variance of the pairwise *difference* under CRN (a pre-specified / theoretical SE, or computed A-side) that alone decides survival-equivalence — the latter is **never** a B-measured level band reused to rank. Both are sentinel-guarded.
- **The harness is the gate Unit 15 reads (contract #1).** Mint an **opaque "oracle-cleared" token** — constructable **only** by this harness on a clean pass — that Unit 15's `solve`-as-recommendation entry point takes as a **required parameter**, so a recommendation that skips validation is a *compile* error, not a runtime discipline check. The token is withheld until **every** check passes on the pinned fixtures: the optimality oracle (all five cases, including the no-change and after-tax-leave-more guards), K-candidate ranking-stability, grade calibration (with the named-driver probe + the deterministic B-family), the held-out-seed defense, **and** the structural pinning clause — every rec-relevant §Strand-5 + healthcare primary pinned (`directionalUntilPinned === false`) and ε calibrated (a still-directional rec-relevant constant or an uncalibrated-ε sentinel withholds the token). This is the structural enforcement of "validation before recommendation," mirroring Act 1's no-unkeyed-write structural seam (the store exposes no write path before a key exists; here the solver exposes no recommendation path before the oracle clears).

**Patterns to follow.** Act 1 seeding/CRN + the externally-derived-golden discipline; `burned/070` (planted-fail self-test — the oracle must be able to fail); `burned/027` (absence-tests need presence companions); `burned/062` (no in-range default fallbacks — the ε sentinel); the "Solver validation" strand of [docs/research/engine-validation-and-tax.md](../research/engine-validation-and-tax.md).

**Test scenarios.**
- Happy path (oracle): the solver's ranking puts the hand-derived known-best drawdown order / bracket-filling conversion optimum **first**; the score margin is reproducible.
- Edge case (cliff-aware oracle): a conversion that fills past an ACA 400%-FPL ceiling or an IRMAA step **ranks below** the cliff-respecting candidate — proving the healthcare overlay flips the ranking, not just the magnitude.
- Edge case (planted wrong-best): a fixture whose declared "best" is actually inferior makes the oracle **fail loud** (`burned/070`) — proving the oracle can detect a wrong recommendation.
- Edge case (K-candidate CRN): all candidates within a seed-set consume normals identical path-for-path across the MFJ→single transition; perturbing one candidate's conversion amount perturbs no other's draws; presence companion confirms ≥1 path crossed each load-bearing boundary (`burned/027`).
- Edge case (grade calibration): the known-robust case grades "just do it"; the known-fragile case grades "coin-flip"; a grade that disagrees with the hand-computed robustness fails the suite.
- Edge case (held-out-seed): selecting on seed-set A and grading on the **same** seed (the curse) yields an over-optimistic grade the held-out-B path corrects — proving B is load-bearing, not decorative.
- Edge case (seed independence): `seedB` derived from `seedA` yields a statistically decorrelated draw matrix; a planted `seedB = seedA + 1` is rejected (a decorrelation check distinct from the U1 period spike).
- Edge case (grade stability): a borderline `(model, goal)` whose grade flips between independent held-out-B draws is detected and forced to the conservative "coin-flip" reading.
- Edge case (ε split): the survival-equivalence set is computed from the selection tie-tolerance (CRN-difference-keyed), never read off the B display band; a level-keyed band wide enough to swallow a CRN-resolvable survival gap (a worse-Tier-1 candidate winning on Tier-2) fails loud.
- Error path (ε sentinel): an uncalibrated ε reads as the out-of-range sentinel and the solver refuses to declare survival-equivalence (fails loud), never silently using an in-range default (`burned/062`).
- Edge case (after-tax leave-more oracle): a fixture where the **gross**-bequest-best strategy is not the **after-tax**-best ranks the after-tax winner first; a solver that argmaxes the gross bequest fails loud (case (iv) — proving the contract-#4 after-tax objective is the one optimized).
- Edge case (no-change oracle): a fixture where conventional drawdown + conversion-0 is the known-best routes to the **no-change state**, never a fabricated active move; the conventional / conversion-0 baseline is asserted present in the candidate set (case (v)).
- Edge case (pick-quality shrinkage) — **lives with Unit 15's `select.ts`, not here** (insight 025: a U14 reference implementation of shrinkage would be the premise ahead of the machinery): a planted near-tie between two **non-conventional** candidates defaults to the conventional (shrunk) pick unless a candidate's advantage survives the shrinkage; a known-dominant strategy survives shrinkage unchanged.
- Edge case (named driver): a near-tie whose ranking flips under the ACA-enhanced toggle emits that named driver; a pure seed-B near-tie emits the `sampling-noise-near-tie` sentinel, never a fabricated input cause.
- Edge case (B-family reproducibility): the grade computed over the `seedB`-derived B-family is byte-identical when the family is re-derived from the same `seedB` (the re-entry reproducibility Unit 17 relies on).
- Error path (directional-fixture gate): a still-directional rec-relevant constant (`directionalUntilPinned === true`) or an uncalibrated ε **withholds** the oracle-cleared token; the `solve`-as-recommendation entry will not type-check / run (contract #1).
- Integration (the gate): Unit 15's solver entry point is unreachable as a *recommendation* path without the harness-minted oracle-cleared token (contract #1).

**Verification.** The recommendation's correctness is testable before any surface renders it: the oracle ranks the known-best case first and fails loud on a planted wrong-best; the ranking is CRN-stable across K candidates over the survivor transition; grades are calibrated against hand-computed robust/fragile cases; the winner is selected on seed-set A and graded on the held-out seed-set B; the ε-band is calibrated (directional-until-pinned) and sentinel-guarded. The harness guarantees the grade is **honest** (held-out, unbiased for whatever was chosen) and that hand-derivable known-best cases **rank first**. An honest grade alone does **not** make a stochastic near-tie *pick* optimal — a "coin-flip"-graded recommendation can still crown a seed-A-overfit winner over an equally-good or better candidate — so **pick-quality defense is IN scope, not deferred**: Unit 15's `select.ts` applies **selection-stage skeptical/Bayesian shrinkage of the seed-A candidate scores toward the conventional-ordering prior** *before* the argmax, and Unit 14 carries the **calibration case** that proves it — a planted near-tie between two non-conventional candidates must default to the conventional (shrunk) pick **unless** a candidate's advantage survives the shrinkage, and a known-dominant strategy must survive it unchanged. (This closes the gap a no-change-vs-conventional safety net alone leaves open: the curse bites hardest among two *aggressive* near-tied candidates, neither near the conventional baseline.) **No Unit-16 surface may treat a solver output as a recommendation until this harness passes** (contract #1).

**Execution note.** Test-first and **first in the act** — the oracle + ranking-stability + calibration fixtures were authored and passing before any *solver* code existed. The one exception is deliberate: `candidates.ts` is enumeration, not search, and it had to be shared so the harness and the search could never score different candidate sets. This is the literal expression of "validation gates the solver."

**Approach.**
- **The search space is bounded and named (R-locked, see [docs/product.md §4 D4](../product.md)):** the **named drawdown policies** `{proportional, taxable-first, pre-tax-first, bracket-fill}` (the Act-1 sequencing substrate) × a **conversion grid `{amount × years}`**. The solver evaluates the **cross-product** of candidates. The grid is not a uniform step: `anchoredConversionAmounts` places each year's points just under every active ACA / IRMAA / bracket rail, read from the shipped `magiLandscape` maps and refined by continuous-monotone bisection, so the resolution is derived from the rails rather than tuned. `taxable-first` is the **conventional** policy the shrinkage prior and the no-change routing are keyed to (`candidates.ts:71`). This is **not** a continuous optimizer (a full continuous optimizer + a "die-with-zero" solver + SS-claiming-age are explicitly chapter-two, deferred). **The grid is cliff-anchored, not uniform — a correctness requirement, not a resolution-tuning knob:** the ACA 400%-FPL cliff and the IRMAA steps are discontinuities whose optimum sits exactly at the just-under-threshold dollar, so each candidate year includes grid points **pinned just under every active ACA/IRMAA threshold plus a local bisection refinement around them**; a uniform grid generically steps over that point and can neither find the just-under-cliff optimum nor avoid recommending a cliff-tripping amount. Relatedly, **`bracket-fill` fills to the *binding effective ceiling* for the year** — the subsidy-aware MAGI line in ACA years, the next IRMAA step post-65, a marginal-rate-aware bracket edge otherwise — **never the nominal tax-bracket top** (which overshoots the cliff the instant healthcare is on). The candidate generation is the **shared `candidates.ts`** (authored in Unit 14, so the harness and the search score the identical set — never two drifting enumerations).
- **The "no-action baseline" is a labeled, scored candidate — the user's CURRENT strategy, not a notional default (contract #6 anchor).** Every Unit-16 comparison (the no-change decision, the rendered delta, the tax-aware reframe) is *"current strategy → recommended,"* so the baseline must be a real candidate scored on the **identical seed-A and seed-B draws** as every other. The search **forces the user's current configured strategy into the candidate set as a labeled baseline**: their current sequencing policy + current conversion, defaulting to `proportional` / conversion-0 if none is set. A returning Act-3 user who set a **custom per-bucket drawdown order** (not one of the four named policies) is handled explicitly — that custom order is evaluated as its own labeled baseline point **outside** the named-policy × grid cross-product (scored on the same draws), so the delta is never silently anchored against the conventional default the user does not actually hold. The conventional-order / conversion-0 candidate is *also* always present (the Unit-14 no-change oracle case (v) and the shrinkage prior both require it).
- **Ranks, does not re-decumulate (the solver-on-top principle).** Each candidate is evaluated by running the **existing** spine + overlays through the **same per-year update function** the manual controls drive (Act-3's `roth.ts` shape) with that candidate's policy + conversion event — so order-of-operations can never drift between the spine, the manual controls, and the solver. **The reduce-to-spine golden invariant is inherited, not re-proven:** with conversion = 0 and the conventional drawdown, a candidate's distribution is byte-identical (same seed) to the validated Trinity/Bengen spine — the golden cases are never perturbed by the solver layer (see [docs/architecture.md §5](../architecture.md)).
- **Identical CRN draws across all candidates (contract #5).** Every candidate, within a seed-set, consumes the **one** market-return draw per year, identical path-for-path — so the ranking is signal, not luck, and asset-location is structurally foreclosed. The K-candidate CRN test (authored in Unit 14) is the proof. The death-order conditional filter carries through unchanged (same seed, same draws, no re-draw). `seedA` is the existing Act-2 seed and `seedB` is its hash/SplitMix-expansion into a well-separated stream (Unit 14) — independent, never `seedA + 1`.
- **The lexicographic objective ≡ the headline metric (contract #4; R21).** **Tier 1** = the survival-floor statistic — essentials covered every year across futures, in the spine's **`X of 10`** metric (the budget's essentials lines, Act 3, are the floor). Candidates within the **selection tie-tolerance** (Unit 14 / contract #2 — the CRN-difference-keyed tolerance computed at selection, **never** the B-measured display band; using B to choose re-contaminates the held-out) are **survival-equivalent**; seed-set B renders the displayed survival number, it does not pick the equivalent set. **Tier 2** = the user's chosen surplus goal, each a **concrete distributional statistic** computed in the same units the headline pivot renders (contract #4). Two rank live: *pay less tax* = lifetime tax paid; *leave more* = the **after-tax-to-heirs** bequest (the first-order §1014/IRD adjustment at the disclosed assumed heir bracket — **never** the gross figure, which can crown the after-tax loser, contract #7). *Live bigger now* is deferred past this unit (contract #4) and joins the goal vocabulary additively when its wiring lands. The objective's quantity **is** the headline's quantity — there is no separate internal score the headline could contradict, which is why `objective.ts` adapts the harness's own ranker rather than re-scoring.
- **Deterministic byte-identical selection (contract #3), with pick-quality shrinkage.** Argmax over many candidates on seed-set A overfits A's noise (the optimizer's curse), so `select.ts` first applies a **deterministic skeptical/Bayesian shrinkage of the seed-A candidate scores toward the conventional-ordering prior** (the Unit-14-calibrated lever for *pick* quality, not just grade honesty) — a candidate only displaces the conventional pick if its advantage **survives** the shrinkage; a known-dominant strategy survives it unchanged. Selection then = quantized lexicographic objective over the shrunk scores + a fixed lexicographic tie-break (policy enum order → conversion amount → years) + a seeded sub-stream **orthogonal** to seed-sets A/B (used only for a residual deterministic tie-break, never for simulation). The winner + the **retained runner-up** (R23 — the runner-up is kept, not discarded, so Unit 16 can show "why this beat it") are reproducible from `(model, seedA, seedB, goal)` alone. No clock/entropy/`Math.random` (Act 1's engine-purity lint enforces it).
- **The 10/10-clamp / surplus regime (feeds contract #6).** When Tier-1 is maxed across futures (survival a given), the objective's *active discriminator* is Tier-2 — the solver emits the surplus winner and flags the regime so Unit 16 can pivot the headline. The clamp is on the *display* of Tier-1; the **selection** still uses the raw pre-clamp Tier-1 to decide survival-equivalence (mirroring Act-3's raw-pre-clamp monotonicity rule). **The surplus-regime flag requires A and B to AGREE, within an over-funded ε** — and "over-funded" is an **ε-tolerance, not an exact 10/10**: the flag trips when the B-rendered Tier-1 is within the display-band ε of the ceiling, so a genuinely over-funded household whose B happens to miss one path by a rounding step is not permanently denied the pivot it most needs. Both disagreement directions are pinned: **(a)** Tier-1 maxed on A (selection) but a real survival gap on B (beyond the over-funded ε) → the flag does **not** trip (no false "safe either way" the displayed B number would contradict); **(b)** B is safe-either-way but the *winner was selected on A's Tier-1 discriminator* (the candidates were not Tier-1-equivalent on A) → the flag does **not** trip either, because pivoting the headline to "survival is a given" while the pick was made on a survival edge would diverge the objective from the headline (contract #4). The pivot fires **only** when both A and B agree the floor is non-binding (the candidates are survival-equivalent on the A-side selection tolerance *and* B is within the over-funded ε of the ceiling); otherwise the survival headline stands.
- **Spending is never a control (contract #8a).** The budget + spending shape (go-go-years front-loading vs level) are **read-only input** to every candidate; the solver moves only sequencing + conversion. No candidate alters a budget line.
- **Candidate-legality filter before scoring (validate-before-mutate, `burned/021`).** Before a `{policy, amount, years}` candidate is scored, each year is checked for legality: past RMD age the year's **RMD is a forced ordinary-income floor taken first and is non-convertible**, so the conversion is clamped to the pre-tax balance remaining **after** the RMD, and a grid amount that exceeds post-RMD convertible headroom (or that would convert in lieu of the RMD) is **rejected as infeasible** (an out-of-range sentinel, never silently scored). The search-space generation owns this — Act 1 enforces the RMD when *running* a candidate, but an infeasible candidate must never reach scoring in the first place.
- **Per-account buckets are a hard precondition of the recommendation (the budget-only-path guard).** The solver searches *sequencing × conversion*, which require per-person **pre-tax / Roth / taxable / HSA** buckets — the second beat **cannot be emitted without them**. Buckets always arrive from the account-level intake (Act 2 / R35), and the intake's account kinds are **mandatory**, so the drafted "budget-only path with no buckets" is unreachable and no `RothAccounts` mini-intake trigger was built. What shipped instead is a **pre-dispatch calm steer**: the store returns `blocked{no-pretax}` for the two reachable shapes — a split household with no pre-tax dollars, and the degenerate no-accounts household — and the surface names that in the household's own frame rather than dispatching. (The steer's first wording was **false on every reachable household**, claiming savings were "entered as one lump sum" — an entry the intake cannot produce; it was reworded 2026-07-23 to name the true shared precondition.) The solver **never** runs on a defaulted bucket split (`burned/062`: a defaulted split is a silent measurement, the calm-but-wrong shape).
- **A chosen Tier-2 goal is an equally hard precondition (the no-discriminator guard).** The lexicographic objective needs a Tier-2 goal to break survival-equivalent ties, so the solve is **never dispatched while `goal === unset-sentinel`**, mirroring the bucket guard. The second beat presents the **`GoalPicker` first** (Unit 16) — "what matters most to you above safety?" — and only dispatches the solve once a goal is set; it never runs a Tier-1-only solve and crowns the deterministic tie-break (policy enum order) as if it were advice. This also closes the **over-funded-with-no-goal** case: for a Tier-1-maxed household an unset goal would leave the solver with no discriminator and nothing to pivot the headline to, so the surplus pivot (contract #6) is likewise unreachable until a goal exists — the goal prompt *is* the first interaction, not a wall after a fabricated recommendation.
- **Solve-once-on-demand + cooperative cancellation (request-epoch).** The interaction is **solve-once-on-demand**, not live-drag: a recommendation is computed when invited (the second beat) or re-invited (a goal re-pick / tune). `cancel.ts` extends the Act-2 request-epoch + Act-3 generation-counter discipline to the **solve** in two shapes. The **commit-epoch guard is unconditional**: a superseded solve never commits, so a fast goal re-pick can never land a stale recommendation, and that guarantee holds whether or not the abort seam fires. The **cooperative abort** is the optional injected seam, checked at the coarse stage boundaries bracketing the two dominant cost centers — before the K-candidate search and before the `m`-draw grade family — so a superseded solve bails there instead of decumulating the whole batch. The engine reads no clock; the caller owns the epoch. **The solve has an explicit calm pending state** (mirroring the spine's pending discipline): while a solve runs, the second-beat surface shows a quiet breathing pending state whose copy names a real duration in plain words — never a spinner, a percentage, or a fabricated progress clock — the prior recommendation (if any) is held visibly-stale-but-labeled rather than blanked, and **no partial or fabricated recommendation paints** before the solve commits; the committed result replaces pending atomically (notify-once). A solve measured in minutes must read as "the tool is working," never as "the tool broke."
- **The WASM measurement gate (the decided trigger, the port deferred).** `profile.ts` measures the real on-demand solve cost: **named policies × conversion grid × the held-out path floor × (SS fixed-point + ACA fixed-point + IRMAA lag), across BOTH seed-sets A and B**, on the reference device. If the on-demand solve **exceeds the budget**, **WASM moves from fast-follow to LOAD-BEARING** — the trigger is *measured, not guessed*; the WASM port itself stays deferred (the TS baseline shipped, the measurement decides the promotion; see the WASM CSP landmine in [docs/architecture.md §9–§10](../architecture.md)). **The dev-laptop worst case measured 72.4 s — 45.9× a single simulate — at 16k paths × 45 years over an 8-candidate roster, healthcare-priced**, and the budget shape came back linear. (The §S6 figure of 48.1 s was a healthcare-blind under-measurement, corrected in the review fold.) **The reference-device measurement is both the WASM trigger and the pin event** for the fallback knobs; that cost is why U17's re-entry gate invites a re-solve rather than running one automatically. Note for the profile: select-on-A + grade-on-B roughly **doubles** the per-candidate evaluation vs a single-seed solve — the profile must measure both, not extrapolate from one. **The profile must run a deliberately compute-worst-case scenario — longest horizon, largest cliff-anchored grid (the most active ACA/IRMAA thresholds → the most pinned points + bisection refinements), both the pre-65 ACA and post-65 IRMAA regimes exercised — not a median one**, or the WASM promotion is decided on a cost a real near-cliff, long-horizon, large-pre-tax household will blow past (the SS/ACA fixed-points converge in 2–3 bounded passes, so the worst case is driven by horizon + cliff-count/grid-size, not iteration depth). The profile budgets **both** seed-sets **and** the `m`-draw held-out B-family (Unit 14), not a single B. **A bounded fallback ladder shipped READY AND FAIL-CLOSED — and it does NOT depend on the deferred WASM port** (`fallback.ts`): the tier + coarse-then-refine shapes and their three knobs exist, `assertFallbackCalibrated` throws on an un-tuned one, and **no shipped path consumes a knob yet** — the core solve still ranks the full set at the base path count. Every knob is sentinel-guarded until measured (`burned/062`): (a) a **coarse-then-refine two-stage search** — a cheap coarse grid prunes the field, and the cliff-anchored just-under-threshold points + bisection are spent **only around the surviving candidates** (the cliff anchoring stays a correctness requirement, but it is applied to survivors, not the full cross-product, so the un-cuttable points don't multiply across every coarse candidate); (b) a stated **candidate-count ceiling** above which (a) engages. The knobs were **pinned 2026-07-22** from the reference-device calibration on **rank stability, never latency**: the interactive path rung landed at 4,000 (rung 1,000 diverged from the 16k-truth crown on both hard cells; 2,000 was the smallest all-match rung, plus one rung of headroom), the ceiling at 5, the coarse-survivor count at 2. **The third rung — a reduced-path interactive solve behind a "full-precision" confirm — was DEFERRED on the record at U16 §S5**: the winner-cannot-flip proof could not be made, so what ships instead is the full-precision solve behind the calm pending state with cooperative cancellation. Nothing ever silently down-samples below the path floor the held-out grade needs.
- **The K-candidate solve-result wire-shape (extends Act 1's worker transfer contract).** Act 1 pins the single-run worker result (typed-array buffers via `Comlink.transfer`, detached on the worker side, fresh per run — see [docs/architecture.md §11](../architecture.md)); the solve returns **K candidates**, so the wire shape is made explicit rather than left to the implementer: the solve returns **one structured payload** carrying the **full seed-B distributions** for the figures Unit 16 renders — the **winner, the retained runner-up, and the no-action baseline** — plus the grade + named-driver + surplus-regime flag, with **only those distributions' buffers in the transfer-list**; the pruned/losing candidates return **scalar lexicographic scores only** (structured-clone, no buffers), never their full distributions. The per-solve allocation is sized for this bounded set (three distributions + scalars), not K full distributions — keeping Act 1's "fresh per run, retains none" discipline true at K-candidate scale.

**Patterns to follow.** Act 1 CRN + the reduce-to-spine invariant; Act-3 `roth.ts` (reuse the per-year update function, never re-implement decumulation; raw-pre-clamp selection); the Act-2 request-epoch / Act-3 generation counters for cooperative cancellation (`burned/005`); `burned/062` (no in-range default — the goal field has an explicit "unset" sentinel, never a silent default goal); `burned/021` (validate-before-mutate — the candidate-legality / RMD-first filter); the §Strand 4/5 + healthcare research.

**Test scenarios.**
- Golden invariant (inherited): a candidate with conversion = 0 + the conventional drawdown is **byte-identical (same seed) to the spine distribution** — the solver layer introduces no spurious delta (the reduce-to-spine anchor).
- Happy path (search): the cross-product of `{proportional, taxable-first, pre-tax-first, bracket-fill}` × the conversion grid is evaluated; a winner + a retained runner-up are returned.
- Edge case (K-candidate CRN): all candidates consume identical normals path-for-path across the MFJ→single transition; a perturbed conversion amount changes one candidate's score without changing any other's draw consumption (contract #5).
- Edge case (lexicographic): two candidates within the **A-side selection tie-tolerance** (the CRN-difference-keyed tolerance, never the B display band — contract #2) are survival-equivalent → the **Tier-2 goal** decides; a candidate with a higher Tier-2 score but a worse Tier-1 (outside the selection tolerance) **never** wins (the floor is absolute — R21); a planted survival-equivalence decided on a seed-B level band fails loud (held-out re-contamination).
- Edge case (objective ≡ headline): the quantity the solver maximizes is the same quantity Unit 16 renders as the headline — a planted divergence (an internal score ≠ the displayed metric) fails loud.
- Edge case (deterministic selection): repeated solves of an unchanged `(model, seedA, seedB, goal)` return a **byte-identical** winner + grade + runner-up; the tie-break is stable, no `Math.random` reachable.
- Edge case (surplus regime): a Tier-1-maxed (over-funded) household → the solver discriminates on Tier-2 and flags the surplus regime for the headline pivot; the raw pre-clamp Tier-1 still governs survival-equivalence; the flag trips only when **A and B agree** within the over-funded ε — a household Tier-1-maxed on A but showing a survival gap on B beyond ε does **not** trip it (no false "safe either way"), and conversely a B-safe household whose winner was *selected on an A-side Tier-1 edge* (candidates not survival-equivalent on A) does **not** trip it either (objective≡headline); a genuinely over-funded household missing the ceiling by a sub-ε rounding step **does** still pivot.
- Edge case (no-action baseline): the user's current configured strategy is present in the candidate set as a labeled baseline scored on the identical seed-A/seed-B draws; a returning user's **custom per-bucket drawdown order** is evaluated as its own baseline point outside the named-policy × grid cross-product; the rendered delta is **current→recommended**, never conventional-default→recommended; the conventional / conversion-0 candidate is also always present.
- Edge case (pick-quality shrinkage): a near-tie between two **non-conventional** candidates resolves to the conventional (shrunk) pick unless an advantage survives shrinkage; a known-dominant strategy is unaffected; the shrunk selection is byte-reproducible.
- Edge case (goal precondition): a solve dispatched while `goal === unset-sentinel` is **refused** — the `GoalPicker` is presented first and no Tier-1-only tie-break is crowned/graded as advice; an over-funded household with no goal sees the goal prompt, not a fabricated surplus pivot.
- Edge case (spending immutable): no candidate alters a budget line; the budget is read-only across the search (contract #8a).
- Edge case (compute fallback): `fallback.test.ts` proves the sentinel bites — `assertFallbackCalibrated` refuses an un-tuned knob rather than routing on a guessed threshold. The three knobs were pinned 2026-07-22 from the reference-device calibration, so the guard no longer fires; the ladder's own routing (coarse-then-refine around survivors, cliff anchoring preserved, never a silent down-sample below the held-out path floor) is built and unconsumed until a shipped path reads it.
- Edge case (cliff-anchored grid): a grid that omits the just-under-cliff point fails to find the cliff-optimal candidate the Unit-14 oracle declares best — proving the grid, not just the ranking, is cliff-aware.
- Edge case (candidate legality): a grid amount exceeding post-RMD convertible headroom in an RMD-active year is rejected as infeasible and never selected; the conversion never substitutes for the RMD.
- Edge case (cancellation): a goal re-pick mid-solve cooperatively aborts the in-flight solve at the coarse stage boundaries that bracket the two dominant cost centers, returning a named `aborted` bin rather than burning that compute; the **unconditional commit-epoch guard** means a superseded solve never commits, so no stale recommendation paints. (Finer per-candidate granularity and the live worker-epoch transport wait on the reference-device numbers.)
- Integration (the gate): the solve entry refuses to emit a *recommendation* without Unit 14's harness-minted oracle-cleared token (contract #1).
- Integration (compute profile): `profile.ts` (pure, injected clock) measures the worst case — named policies × grid × the path floor × (SS + ACA + IRMAA) across **both** seed-sets and the `m`-draw B-family — and reports whether the on-demand budget is exceeded (the WASM trigger). The dev-laptop run came back at 72.4 s / 45.9× a single simulate.

**Verification.** The solver ranks the bounded policy × conversion-grid space on identical CRN futures, selects a byte-identical winner + retained runner-up under a lexicographic objective whose metric is the headline metric, reduces byte-identically to the validated spine when off, never touches spending, cancels cooperatively on a re-pick, and reports a measured (compute-worst-case) profile that decides the WASM promotion — its conversion grid is **cliff-anchored** (contains the just-under-cliff optimum), every candidate clears the **RMD-first legality filter**, and the survival-equivalent set is decided by the CRN-difference selection tolerance (never the B display band) — and it does none of this as a *recommendation* until Unit 14's harness passes.

Fabricating an active recommendation when no change beats the status quo is the recommender's cardinal-sin shape. **Precedence with the surplus pivot:** when the surplus regime is active **and** the winner is within the selection tolerance of the baseline on Tier-2, the two states **compose** into a **no-dollar** reassurance — the shipped line is *"You're already on one of the strongest paths we tested — nothing else we tried looks likely to pull clearly ahead"* — **never** the active "keeps ~$X more" claim (which would fabricate a surplus gain that does not exist) and **never** a "safe either way" absolute (ruling 7's veto). The word *already* is what carries the relief, and it is only true because `noChange` was **re-keyed 2026-08-03** to mean "the crowned plan IS the one you already run", compared by plan rather than by candidate index — under the old conventional-baseline keying this line told a `proportional` household they were already on a strong path while the recommendation was to switch off their order.
- **Confidence grading on the headline (contract #7; R22).** The grade rides the **headline**, never buried in tapped-away math. The harness's enum ids (`just-do-it` / `coin-flip`) stay **internal and never render**; what the household reads is the terse shipped pair — *"A confident lean"* / *"A close call"* — with the coin-flip's hinge note beside it. The grade is the **calibrated** grade from Unit 14's held-out-seed reporting — the surface renders it, it does not recompute robustness. The "coin-flip" render names *what it hinges on* (the near-tie's sensitive input — e.g. "this depends on whether the enhanced ACA subsidies return"), turning fragility into honesty rather than wishy-washiness. Depth-on-demand must never invert into certainty-on-top / caveats-below. The *"what it hinges on"* line requires a **sensitive-input attribution distinct from the seed-B margin grade** — a seed-B dispersion cannot name *which input* a ranking hinges on, and a legislative/scenario sensitivity (the ACA-enhanced toggle) is invisible to it — so **Unit 14's `gradeCalibration` sensitivity probe emits the named driver** (re-ranking the top candidates under the ACA-enhanced toggle + a fixture-vintage perturbation, reporting the single input whose flip moves the grade) alongside the grade; the surface only renders it, and a pure seed-B near-tie carries the `sampling-noise-near-tie` sentinel — a *sampling*-framed hinge, never a fabricated input cause.

*A saved rec is a real action, so it is only spoken in the present tense when the build can still stand behind it.*

**Goal.** Treat a saved recommendation as the real action it is: on re-entry, decide whether the remembered verdict may still be spoken in the present tense, and when it may not, say so plainly and offer a re-solve the household chooses. Never re-present a superseded ranking as current, and never spend minutes of solve on someone's behalf without being asked.

**Requirements.** R7 (the saved recommendation's inputs/reasoning re-surface), R10 (the recommendation re-presents honestly on return), R24 (ACA legislative volatility re-verified), R25 (a superseded recommendation acted on is the cardinal-sin shape — calm-but-wrong over time).

**Dependencies.** **Unit 15** (the re-solve path), **Unit 16** (the recommendation surface + grade render + the reserved save slot), Act 3 (the per-surface staleness map + the returning-user re-entry), Act 1 (the encrypted store + the record's persistence — see [docs/architecture.md §7.3](../architecture.md)), `src/engine/constants/` (the year-keyed fixtures whose vintage drives staleness).

**Files (as built).**

Deliberately **not** the planned separate `recStaleness.ts`: it does not re-implement the clock logic, it runs the *same* `deriveStaleness` against a scenario whose five era-bearing fields are overlaid from the record. - `src/store/savedRecommendationMint.ts` — the mint, which validates the in-memory record before it can be written.

Dev plants `?vault=rec` / `?vault=recold` / `?vault=datearrived` drive the real-browser arms, and the record made the saved recommendation's first trip through real WebCrypto and IndexedDB.

**Approach.**
- **The three-conjunct re-entry gate (contract #8b).** The drafting premise was an *inversion* of Act 3's spine rule — the spine supposedly re-presenting a saved verdict under its saved vintage while a recommendation always re-solved under current fixtures. **That premise is refuted (ruling 6): the spine never re-presents under a saved vintage either.** It always recomputes under current rules, and when no clock has fired the persisted seed makes that recompute byte-identical to what the household saw (`staleness.ts:17-21`). So what shipped is not an inversion but a **stricter admission test**, in `savedRecommendation.ts`: the remembered verdict may be spoken in the present tense **only when all three hold** — (1) the freshly-built solver-run fingerprint matches the record's, (2) `SOLVER_CODE_VERSION` matches the record's, (3) no rulebook clock fired against the record's own `era`. Fail any one and the card renders **superseded**, naming **every** cause that holds, never just the first found (insight 101: a warning that names one cause when two hold describes its poster child, not the predicate).
- **Conjunct 3 compares the RECORD's era, not the scenario's — this is the correctness reason the era snapshot exists.** `deriveStaleness` reads the *scenario's* vintage stamps, and `scenarioFromDraft` re-mints every one of them at every save. A plan whose recommendation was found under old brackets and then re-saved after a bracket change carries stamps reading "saved under today's rules": the scenario-side compare would report no clock fired while the remembered verdict was priced under superseded law. That is the constant-blind hole one level up — `solverRunFingerprint` excludes the consumed constants **by design** — and re-presenting a superseded ranking as current advice with a proof attached is the cardinal calm-but-wrong sin. A record that cannot carry its era is **refused at the codec** and dropped through the non-fatal atom: the household loses a memory rather than hearing a stale ranking in the present tense.
- **The re-solve is INVITED, never automatic.** The re-open control names the outcome it leads to ("Work out the current strategy") and **names its cost** ("This can take a few minutes"), because a solve was measured at 45.9× a single simulate and a control that hides that is an invitation the household cannot price. Nothing re-solves on unlock.
- **The superseded copy is about the ADVICE, never about the display.** The lead reads ***"It may no longer fit the two of you"***, with one clause per cause beneath it: *your numbers have changed* · *there isn't enough in your plan right now to line it up against* · *the way strategies are worked out has changed* · *the tax and health-cost rules have moved*. Two wordings are **banned by what they cost**. It shipped for a while as *"We can no longer show this as current"*, which reads as a claim about our rendering — a household still executing the saved conversions infers the display is stale and the strategy stands; on `?vault=recold` every cause clause sits below the 791px fold, so that sentence was the *only* thing in frame (cold-read card 7, hard-flag blocker). And the tidy mirror *"It no longer matches your plan"* is **false on the `inputs-unavailable` cause**, which says the opposite — that we cannot line it up at all. The hedge in "may no longer fit" is load-bearing rather than soft: the line must be true across five arms, the four named causes plus the fail-closed broken-contract arm where the card renders it alone over an empty list.
- **The holds line claims conjunct 1 and nothing more.** It reads ***"It still lines up with the numbers you've entered"*** — fingerprint identity, the sole computed guarantee behind that face. **The universal negative is banned**: "nothing has moved since then that would change it" is *rosier* than the three conjuncts support, because the fingerprint excludes constant vintages by design and a nameless re-base is deliberately absent from `rulesMoved` — a rulebook can move while the params stay byte-identical. The line also names **the data, not the plan of action**: "matches your plan" invited the inference that the reassurance was about what the household was *doing*. It is not, and it cannot be: `drawdownPolicy` is inside the fingerprint, so a household that **acts** on a sequencing recommendation trips `inputs-changed` and gets the superseded card. Taking the advice demotes the memory; ignoring it earns the reassurance.
- **The card is MINIMAL by ruling, and the remembered figures stay unquoted.** It carries four things: that a saved read exists, how old it is, whether it still holds, and the way back. The grade and verdict enums are **persisted but never quoted** — a card that speaks a remembered figure as if it were current is what the preserved council dissent aims at — and leaving them out means the card can grow later with no schema change. **What was specified here and did NOT ship: the "same-strategy-degraded" copy register** (the re-solve returning the same strategy with a fallen grade or a flipped delta sign), the **retroactive-ACA past-cost note**, and the **gate-red branching on the token's withheld reason**. Each needs the card to speak a *re-solved* verdict, and the shipped card does not re-solve — it hands the household the invited control instead. None of the three appears in `docs/backlog.md`, so they are unshipped and currently untracked; the reasoning for each is kept below because whoever files them will need it.
- **What the record's era carries, and what carries the rest.** The `rules-changed` conjunct runs `deriveStaleness` over five era-bearing stamps overlaid from the record — `appDefaultVersion`, `taxVintageDetail`, `stateTaxVintage`, `healthcareVintage`, `dateVintage` — each read from the canonical `src/engine/constants/` producers, so no fixture vintage is re-typed here. The **RMD-age rule** and the **senior-bonus sunset** are not separate clocks: the RMD banded table is **birth-year-keyed and static** in the constants, and the senior bonus is priced only inside its own `effectiveFrom … sunsetAfter` calendar window, so in both cases a re-derivation under any wall clock prices the identical rule and a real change to either arrives as a new `legalBasis`/`taxYear` and fires the tax clock (ruling 6; `staleness.ts:33-47`). Every ranking-affecting *household* input — the per-bucket balances, the goal, the heir bracket, the budget, the horizon, the people, the overlay flags, the ordered candidate roster — is covered instead by conjunct 1's fingerprint, which is why a drifted pre-tax pool or an advanced budget window demotes the record without needing a clock of its own. And because the winner is reproducible only given fixed ranking code, conjunct 2 compares `SOLVER_CODE_VERSION` with `!==` rather than `<`: a record from a **newer** build is equally un-re-presentable, because this build cannot reproduce that ranking either. Fail-closed in both directions.
- **Re-verify the ACA legislative status — it can flip the whole model (contract #8b; R24; healthcare §2).** The enhanced-ACA-subsidy status is **live, possibly-retroactive policy**; if the enhanced regime is restored (possibly retroactively), the **400% FPL cliff disappears** and the pre-65 conversion calculus **flips** — which can **invert which strategy wins**. The ACA status rides the `healthcareVintage` stamp inside the record's era, so a saved recommendation computed under the 2026 cliff-on base case is superseded the moment the legislative status moves. (The build-time re-verify of the status itself is a roadmap validation gate, `verify:aca`; this unit consumes its result.) One clock is deliberately **quiet**: `acaVerifiedOn` is excluded from the healthcare compare, because a re-verify that confirms the *same* law is provenance rather than drift, and firing on it would stale every vault monthly — alarm-when-fine is a lie in the safe direction, and still a lie. A real regime change moves `acaStatus`, the coverage year, or the numbers, and those all fire. **What is NOT built: the retroactive arm.** The enhanced regime could be restored *possibly retroactively to 2026* (healthcare §2), so a past action's cost can change after the fact, which a forward supersession does not address. The specified answer was a distinct generic note — *"a recent law change may apply to a past year, so an action you may already have taken could now carry a different cost — worth revisiting with a pro"* — phrased generically because neither execution nor tax-year is tracked. No such copy exists in the catalog.
- **The gate-red-at-re-entry fallback is SPECIFIED, NOT BUILT — and the reasoning is why it should be.** An invited re-solve calls Unit 15, which refuses to emit a recommendation while the oracle-cleared token is absent (contract #1). The token is withheld for **more than one reason** (`WithheldReason`, `oracleToken.ts:43`), and the fallback was to **branch on WHY** rather than always blaming the law: **(a)** the ACA legislative status is unverified — re-verified every build, so it can be unverified exactly when the law is in flux, the case this unit most cares about → *"we can no longer stand behind this and cannot currently re-verify the law — do not act on it until the status is confirmed"*; **(b)** a rec-relevant primary is still directional or ε is uncalibrated → *"this recommendation isn't validated on this version yet — don't act on it until it is"*. Neither the re-open path nor a stale re-presentation is legal in that state, so the saved strategy must be shown clearly marked with the matching reason, never silently suppressed and never blamed on the law when a primary is merely un-pinned. The engine side exists — the withheld-reason enum names the true reason and U16 renders a withheld state on the live surface — but **the record card has no such branch**: its four causes are the trichotomy's, and a token withheld at re-open time is not among them.
- **No serialization sentinel was needed, because the record holds no dollars.** The specified landmine was a saved "expected surplus = never depleted" field silently becoming `null` through `JSON.stringify`/IndexedDB (`archive/do-not-disturb/009`). The record that shipped is **enumerated, never a dollar** — grade, sub-tenth qualifier, `noChange`, `surplusRegime`, an opaque `candidateId` — so the class was designed out rather than guarded. The one numeric field, `mintedAt`, carries the same finite-integer epoch-**day** unit and codec range gate as `savedAt`, because an epoch-*millisecond* value there is a finite integer that reads as year ~55000 (DND 009, insight 046). **Execution status is not tracked at all**, and nothing in the record claims the action was taken.
- **The v3 write fires on exactly one event: the first explicit save of a recommendation** — never on a goal-pick, never on a solve, never on an un-saved hypothetical. On an **existing on-disk vault** it goes through Act-1's **model-only re-encrypt** (same DK, fresh IV, wraps untouched), the same atomic path Act 3 uses for v1→v2, so an **aborted upgrade still decrypts the intact prior vault** — a half-written vault is the same catastrophic class as Act-1's first-save-atomicity landmine. **A brand-new household whose first-ever Save *is* a saved recommendation** (intake → budget → recommendation-invite → buckets → goal → commit, with nothing prior on disk) mints a v3 blob directly through Act-1's keyed first-save path: the first Save writes whatever `schemaVersion` the in-memory shape implies, and the re-encrypt *upgrade* path is only for an existing vault advancing versions, never a precondition for reaching v3.
- **What a re-solve, once invited, must preserve.** It runs Unit 15 on the **saved goal** and the household's own scenario seed, so the futures are continuous with what the household saw rather than a fresh random scenario, but under the **current** fixtures and the **current confirmed** balances and budget — never the saved pre-confirm balances. Continuity of futures costs nothing to keep, because `seedB` is derived from `seedA` rather than stored: re-deriving it is byte-exact. **Reusing that hold-out under changed fixtures does NOT contaminate it, even when the candidate set's size or shape changed** (a moved grid resolution, RMD age, or feasibility): the hold-out's validity comes from `seedB ⊥ seedA` (contract #2), **not** from any binding to a particular candidate set — it is an independent *scenario* set, not a per-candidate object, so a winner selected on A and graded on the B-family over the *current* candidates is unbiased regardless. The invariant that must hold: the grade is **recomputed** over the current candidates and never re-presented from the record, and it still **selects on A, grades on B** — never selects on B.

**Test scenarios.**
- Happy path (holds): a saved recommendation whose fingerprint, solver version and record-era clocks are all unchanged renders the holds line, and that line claims fingerprint identity only — a planted universal negative ("nothing has moved") fails the suite.
- Edge case (rules moved): a moved tax-table vintage renders the superseded card with the rules clause, the lead line about the **advice** rather than the display, and the invited re-open naming its cost. Nothing re-solves on its own.
- Edge case (era, not scenario): a record found under old brackets and then **re-saved** after a bracket change is still superseded — the scenario's re-minted stamps must not launder it. A compare against the scenario's stamps fails the suite.
- Edge case (every cause, not the first): a record failing two conjuncts renders both clauses (insight 101).
- Edge case (inputs changed): a drifted per-bucket split trips `inputs-changed` through the fingerprint. So does **acting on the advice** — `drawdownPolicy` is inside the fingerprint, so taking a sequencing recommendation demotes the memory.
- Edge case (unanswerable): no fresh fingerprint can be built (the goal is unset, a required fact is missing) → `inputs-unavailable`, and the card never answers "yes" to an identity question it cannot answer.
- Edge case (solver version): a record from a **newer** build is superseded exactly as one from an older build — the compare is `!==`, never `<`.
- Edge case (quiet clocks): an `acaVerifiedOn` re-verify that confirms the same law does **not** demote the record; a real regime change does.
- Edge case (era unavailable): a record that cannot carry all five era fields is refused at the codec and dropped through the non-fatal atom — the household loses the memory rather than hearing a stale ranking.
- Edge case (empty superseded): the superseded arm with zero cause clauses still renders a non-colour marker and the lead line, never a heading over nothing.
- Edge case (fold): on `?vault=recold` the lead line's bottom sits in frame at 1536×791 — `vertical-fit.spec.ts` asserts it on both plants and is mutation-proven red at two lines.
