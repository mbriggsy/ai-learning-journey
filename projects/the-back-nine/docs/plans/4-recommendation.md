## The eight cross-cutting contracts

*These eight contracts span the four units below. They are named once here so a reader sees the seams, then enforced in the unit that owns each — mirroring the contract sets that govern the earlier acts. Several are seams the earlier acts explicitly handed forward into an Act-4 home — the validated spine + CRN draw schedule (Act 1), the tax-and-accounts + healthcare overlays (Act 1 / Act 3), the `memoryModel` orchestrator + request-epoch (Act 2), the `require-the-hedge` lint (Act 3), and the per-surface staleness map (Act 3); this section is that home.*

1. **Validation gates the solver — U14 is built and PASSING before U15 is allowed to recommend (owned cross-cutting by Units 14→15; the recommendation half of the SC two-tier correctness contract).** The success criteria are two-tier: the engine's *number* is right (Trinity/Bengen golden cases, Act 1) **and** the solver's *recommendation* is right (the "Solver validation" strand of [docs/research/engine-validation-and-tax.md](../research/engine-validation-and-tax.md)). Unit 14 is the second tier and it **gates** Unit 15: the **optimality/ranking oracle** (hand-computable known-best drawdown/conversion cases), **ranking-stability under CRN**, and **grade calibration** must each exist and pass before the solver may emit a recommendation to any surface. This is the discipline that makes a confidently-wrong recommendation **fail loud**, the way Trinity/Bengen make "the number is right" testable. Until the oracle passes, any solver output is **directional** (marked so in code) and no Unit-16 surface may render it as a recommendation. **The order is structural, not a convention — and structural means *type-level*, not a boolean:** the "oracle-cleared" result is an **opaque nominal token** constructable **only** by the validation harness on a clean pass, and Unit 15's `solve`-as-recommendation entry point takes that token as a **required parameter** — so shipping a recommendation without a passing oracle is a TypeScript *compile* error, never a discipline failure (mirroring Act 1's no-unkeyed-write seam, where no write path *exists* without a key). The gate's own test plants a wrong-best case the oracle must reject (`burned/070`). **`fixtures-pinned` is itself a checked clause of the token, not an assumption:** the harness mints the cleared token **only when every rec-relevant §Strand-5 + healthcare primary the near-ties depend on is pinned (`directionalUntilPinned === false`) *and* ε is calibrated** (contract #2) — a test plants a single still-directional rec-relevant constant (or an uncalibrated-ε sentinel) and asserts the token is **refused** and the solver refuses to recommend, so the gate can never go green on the directional fixtures that decide the very near-ties the optimizer overfits.

2. **The held-out-seed discipline — select on A, grade on B (owned by Unit 14; consumed by Units 15+16).** Argmax over many candidates on a single seed overfits *that seed's* noise (the optimizer's curse), so the in-sample winner's score is optimistically biased. The defense: **select** the winning strategy on **seed-set A**, then **report its graded confidence on an independent held-out seed-set B**. **Both seed-sets are themselves CRN-correct** — within A, all K candidates share the one-draw-per-year schedule (contract #5); within B, the same. A and B must be **statistically independent, not merely "distinct"**: `seedA` is the existing Act-2-minted scenario seed (so the spine headline still reproduces under it — Act 1), and `seedB` is derived by a **hash / SplitMix expansion of `seedA` into a well-separated stream** — never `seedA + 1` or a near-integer, whose mulberry32 streams correlate and silently make B optimistic. This is seed-*input* decorrelation only; it does **not** replace mulberry32 as the path PRNG. Both seeds are first-class persisted model fields surviving the encrypt→decrypt round-trip bit-identically (persist `seedA` + the expanded `seedB`; no third master is stored). The **survival-equivalent ε has two distinct roles that must not be conflated:** the **display band** (the rendered uncertainty interval) is measured on **B**; the **selection tie-tolerance** that declares two candidates survival-equivalent is a *selection* decision and must **never** be a B-measured level band — using B to choose re-contaminates the held-out. The selection tolerance is a pre-specified / theoretical standard error (or computed A-side), keyed to the variance of the pairwise **difference** between candidates under CRN (which CRN shrinks far below the variance of the level — a level-keyed band over-declares ties and can drop a survival-relevant gap down into Tier-2). Deciding the near-ties on the selection seed's noise *is* the curse. Compounded landmine: directional-until-pinned fixtures decide the exact near-ties the optimizer overfits, so **pinning the §Strand-5 + healthcare primaries is a hard solver PREREQUISITE**, not a residual gate.

3. **Deterministic, byte-identical recommendation selection (owned by Unit 15; the recommendation analogue of Act 1's seed/headline identity).** The selected strategy + its grade + its runner-up must be **byte-identical across reproductions and across re-entry** — a recommendation that jitters between runs cannot be trusted with money. Selection = **a quantized objective** (the lexicographic score rounded to a stable display/decision granularity so float noise can't reorder near-ties) + **a deterministic lexicographic tie-break** (a fixed candidate ordering: policy enum order, then conversion amount, then years) + **a seeded sub-stream ORTHOGONAL to the simulation draws** (a third stream, never one of seed-sets A/B, used only where a tie-break needs a deterministic coin — never for simulation, so it cannot perturb any draw schedule). No `Math.random`, no clock, no `getRandomValues` inside the solver (the engine-purity lint of Act 1 already forbids them in `src/engine/**`). The selection is reproducible from `(model, seedA, seedB, goal)` alone. **Byte-identical is a same-JS-engine guarantee** (Act 1 scopes the cross-engine reproduction claim and owns the quantized-headline guard that makes the *displayed* `X of 10` robust to a last-ULP transcendental difference across browser engines): the selection run and the held-out-B grade for one recommendation must execute on the **same engine instance**, never a precomputed cross-engine baseline, or a single ULP delta in one `Math.exp` could reorder a near-tie and silently bias the grade.

4. **Objective ≡ headline metric, lexicographic (owned by Unit 15 for the objective; Unit 16 for the render).** The objective is **lexicographic** (R21): **Tier 1 (the floor)** = the survival-floor statistic — essentials covered every year across futures, expressed in the spine's **`X of 10`** natural-frequency metric; **Tier 2 (the user picks)** = a surplus goal, **each a concrete distributional statistic the headline renders directly** (there is no separate internal score the headline could contradict): **_pay less tax_** = lifetime tax paid (real dollars); **_leave more_** = the bequest measured **after-tax to heirs** — the gross estate adjusted by a *first-order* §1014 / IRD treatment (taxable basis stepped up at death, inherited pre-tax taxed as ordinary income-in-respect-of-a-decedent at a **disclosed assumed heir bracket**, Roth tax-free), **never gross / pre-heir-tax** (a gross objective can crown the strategy that leaves *less* after-tax — contract #7); **_live bigger now_** = the held-out **`X of 10` confidence that the user's chosen front-loaded (go-go-years) discretionary spending shape holds across futures** — a confidence-over-*fixed*-spending statistic, **never a spend-amount the solver raises** (the budget stays read-only, contract #8a; telling the user how much *more* they could sustainably spend is a continuous-spending / die-with-zero solver, explicitly chapter-two). **The quantity the solver maximizes IS the quantity the headline shows** — Tier-1 score ≡ the survival headline, and when survival is a given the headline pivots (contract #6) to the **same** Tier-2 quantity the solver optimized — so a recommendation can never recommend a move that worsens its own hero number. Candidates within the **selection tie-tolerance** (the CRN-difference-keyed tolerance of contract #2, computed at selection — never the B-measured *display* band) are **survival-equivalent**; among them the winner is the best Tier-2 score. Seed-set B carries the displayed number + the grade, **never** the choice of which candidates are survival-equivalent. **Spending shape is never an objective term** (contract #8) — the solver optimizes *funding*, not how you live.

5. **The single shared market draw per year — now MORE load-bearing (owned by Unit 15; one-line extension of Act 1's CRN contract + Act 3's draw contract).** All account buckets (pre-tax / Roth / taxable / **HSA**) share **one** market-return draw per year; buckets differ only in tax treatment, never in return assumption. This is what lets the solver rank **K candidate strategies on identical futures** (signal, not luck) and it **structurally forecloses asset-location**. Act 3 proved this for a 2-arm Roth what-if; **Unit 15 generalizes the CRN test to K candidates** — every policy × conversion-grid candidate, within a seed-set, consumes the normals **identical path-for-path across the survivor MFJ→single transition**. Per-bucket draws are forbidden (they break CRN and re-open asset-location). The death-order **conditional filter** carries through unchanged: same seed, same draws, no re-draw.

6. **The recommend-second flow + the 10/10→surplus headline pivot (owned by Unit 16; consumes the Act-2 spine answer + the Act-3 budget two-tier reading).** The spine confidence answer is the **first** beat (the magic moment, unchanged — never overwritten). The recommendation is the immediate **second** beat — *"and here's what we'd do about it"* — with the comparative reasoning (why this beat the runner-up) **one tap down** (depth-on-demand, R4). The user **tunes / overrides / re-picks the goal**, both futures updating. It is **calm and invited**, never a nagging alert/badge/engagement bait (R11). **The 10/10 clamp pivot:** when survival is a given (over-funded household / Tier-1 already maxed across futures), the headline honestly **switches** to the chosen surplus metric — *"you're safe either way; this keeps more from the IRS"* — so the solver's signal is never eaten by the survival clamp. The active metric is always rendered explicitly so the user never silently re-bases between the spine beat and the recommendation beat.

7. **The hedge-on-headline lint + the disclosed-omission rule (owned by Unit 16; consumes the Act-3 `require-the-hedge` lint + the Act-2 copyGuard catalog).** Every recommendation **grades its own confidence** and **the hedge rides the headline** — depth-on-demand must never invert into certainty-on-top / caveats-hidden-below (R22). copyGuard's **`require-the-hedge`** lint (a *positive/require* assertion introduced in Act 3, not the legacy ban-list) is extended to assert the recommendation headline + the grade copy each wear their probabilistic hedge by construction; certainty verbs stay banned (R12). All recommendation strings route through the `src/ui/copy.ts` catalog (the recommendation viz in `src/viz` stays string-free, receiving text as props — `burned/063`). **Disclosed-omission rule:** because a disclosed omission can **invert a ranking** (not just blunt a delta), ACA-PTC + IRMAA are already **IN** the model (Act 1), and the remaining out-but-disclosed effects (**NIIT, state tax**) are disclosed in plain language **adjacent to the delta**, never silently dropped. One further effect is **lever-*sensitive*** and so is handled separately from the lever-inert NIIT/state pair: when the chosen Tier-2 goal is *leave more*, a death-time **§1014 basis step-up** (taxable stepped up at death, inherited pre-tax fully taxable to heirs as IRD, Roth tax-free) **moves with the sequencing/conversion lever** and can invert which strategy leaves more *after-tax* to heirs. By the IN/OUT line's **own falsifiable test** (the lever moves which account is preserved into the estate, *and* the effect can invert the ranking) it therefore belongs **IN**, not disclosed-and-omitted — a disclosure cannot fix a ranking the solver argmaxed on the wrong quantity (gross), which is the calm-but-wrong recommendation shape this whole act exists to forbid. So the *leave more* objective is the **after-tax-to-heirs** bequest (contract #4): a **first-order** §1014 / IRD adjustment is modeled into the bequest statistic, with the **assumed heir tax bracket** carried as a *disclosed, R7-editable assumption* (surfaced adjacent to the leave-more delta) rather than the unmodeled gross figure. The lever-**inert** **NIIT + state tax** remain the only OUT-but-disclosed effects (neither moves which account is preserved); the full-fidelity estate model — multiple heirs, state estate tax, trusts, the precise IRD timing — stays chapter-two.

8. **Spending is never solver-recommended (owned by Unit 15) + the stale-rec inversion (owned by Unit 17).** **(a)** The solver's controls are **only** the two funding levers — named drawdown policies × the conversion grid; the **budget / spending shape is user-set input** (the floor essentials + the discretionary surplus the funding is optimized for), never a control the solver moves. A tool must never recommend a *lifestyle*; it shows honest consequences. **(b)** A saved recommendation is a **real, executed action**, so its staleness rule **INVERTS** the spine's Act-3 rule. The spine re-*presents* a saved verdict under its **saved** fixture vintage (so the number matches the screenshot) and only re-*runs* under current fixtures on demand. A recommendation cannot do that: a stale saved rec **always re-solves under CURRENT fixtures**, and the staleness copy reads ***"the action we recommended may no longer be advised,"*** **not** *"your number drifted."* Re-verifying the **ACA legislative status** is part of this (it can flip the whole pre-65 model and thus which strategy wins). A saved *manual-control* result (an Act-3 what-if, not an executed action) keeps the spine's re-present-under-saved-vintage rule; **only a saved *recommendation* inverts** — the boundary the reset turns on (a saved control what-if ≠ a saved executed action).

*The optimality oracle, ranking-stability, grade calibration, the held-out-seed defense.*

**Goal.** Make the *recommendation's correctness* testable before the solver is allowed to speak — a hand-computable known-best oracle, K-candidate ranking-stability under CRN, calibrated confidence grades, and the held-out-seed defense against the optimizer's curse — so a confidently-wrong recommendation **fails loud**.

**Requirements.** R22 (grade calibration), R25 (the cardinal honesty bar — calm-but-wrong is the sin, the bar rises for a recommender), SC (the recommendation half of the two-tier correctness criterion).

**Dependencies.** Act 1's validated spine + CRN draw schedule (the oracle runs through it), the tax-and-accounts overlay (the conversion/sequencing cases evaluate against it), the healthcare overlay — ACA fixed-point + IRMAA lag — the near-ties depend on, and `src/engine/constants/` (the year-keyed table; **the §Strand-5 + healthcare primaries pinned** is a hard prerequisite — contract #2). *Has no dependency on Unit 15 — it is built first, by construction.* **The candidate enumerator is shared, not duplicated:** the policy × cliff-anchored-conversion-grid candidate generation (with the RMD-first legality filter) lives in **`src/engine/solver/candidates.ts`, created here in Unit 14** (because `rankingStability.ts` must run the CRN check over the *same* candidate set Unit 15 later scores) and **imported by both** U14's harness and U15's `search.ts` — so "built before any solver code" is honest and the two can never drift to different candidate sets.

**Files.**
- Create: `src/engine/solver/candidates.ts` (the **shared** policy × cliff-anchored-grid candidate enumerator + RMD-first legality filter — imported by both this harness and Unit 15's `search.ts`, so they never drift), `src/engine/validation/optimalityOracle.ts` (the known-best drawdown/conversion cases — the five cases below), `src/engine/validation/rankingStability.ts` (the K-candidate CRN ranking check, run over `candidates.ts`), `src/engine/validation/gradeCalibration.ts` (robust-vs-fragile calibration + the shrinkage calibration case + the named-driver sensitivity probe), `src/engine/validation/heldOutSeed.ts` (the seed-set A/B harness + the deterministic B-family + the ε-band measurement), `src/engine/reference/solver-cases/` (**committed**, hand-derived oracle fixtures — never generated; marked **directional-until-pinned** to the §Strand-5/healthcare primaries, the same discipline as Trinity/Bengen; **each fixture stores its preconditions** — constant-rate; RMD / SS / cap-gains / cliff / bequest each on or off — and the harness refuses to apply a fixture's known-best outside its declared preconditions)
- Modify: `src/engine/constants/` (the **survival-equivalent ε constant** is *output* by this unit's calibration and stored here as an out-of-range-sentinel-guarded value — see Approach), `src/shared/model.ts` (the second scenario seed `seedB` field — **net-new** — alongside the spine's **existing** seed: the field Act 2 already mints, now read as `seedA`, **not a new field** — so the spine headline still reproduces under "the" seed; only `seedB` is added, under the Act-4 `schemaVersion`-3 bump — contract #2 / Unit 17)
- Test: `src/engine/validation/__tests__/optimalityOracle.test.ts`, `rankingStability.test.ts`, `gradeCalibration.test.ts`, `heldOutSeed.test.ts`

**Approach.**

- **The optimality oracle = hand-computable known-best cases.** Build cases where the best drawdown/conversion order is *known by independent derivation*, not by the engine's own formula (a golden the engine computes via its own path proves typing, not correctness — see the externally-derived-fixture discipline in [docs/architecture.md §5](../architecture.md)):
  - **(i)** the conventional **taxable → tax-deferred → Roth** drawdown ordering is optimal **only in a constant-marginal-rate world** — a flat rate, or income high enough that every year sits in one bracket, with **no RMD, no SS taxation, no cap-gains stacking, no ACA/IRMAA cliff, and no bequest motive** — these stated as the fixture's gating preconditions. (A *low-other-income* world is explicitly **not** this case: it is the bracket-filling regime where (ii), not conventional ordering, is the hand-derived best — so the oracle must never be hardcoded to conventional ordering there.)
  - **(ii)** a **bracket-filling Roth conversion optimum** — fill to the top of a target bracket and no further — in a **stripped** case where **SS taxation, cap-gains/QD stacking, and all cliffs are OFF**, so the in-bracket marginal rate is genuinely flat and "bracket top" actually equals "today's marginal = future marginal" (the real rule is marginal-rate equalization; "bracket top" is a coincidence of the simplified world, never the rule the solver should learn).
  - **(iii)** a **cliff-aware case** where filling past an ACA 400%-FPL ceiling or an IRMAA step **loses**, so the healthcare-blind ranking is sign-inverted (the tax-blind delta makes aggressive conversion look better than it is) — here the binding ceiling is the subsidy-aware MAGI / IRMAA-step line, **not** the bracket top, so (ii) and (iii) are **separate worlds** (healthcare off vs on) and a fixture's known-best is never applied across that boundary.
  - **(iv)** a **leave-more after-tax case** where the **gross**-bequest-best strategy is *not* the **after-tax**-best — the §1014 step-up / IRD treatment (contract #4 / #7) inverts the ranking (e.g. preserving a taxable bucket that steps up at death beats converting a pre-tax bucket the heirs would owe IRD on at the assumed heir bracket) — so the oracle's known-best is the **after-tax-to-heirs** winner and a solver that argmaxes the gross bequest **fails loud**; this is the hand-derived guard that the after-tax objective is the one actually optimized.
  - **(v)** a **no-change case** where the conventional drawdown + zero conversion *is* the known-best for the `(model, goal)`: the oracle asserts the solver routes to the **no-change state** (Unit 16) and does **not** crown a fabricated active move — which requires the search space to **always** contain the conventional-order / conversion-0 candidate as a labeled baseline (Unit 15).
  - The oracle asserts the solver's **ranking** puts the known-best candidate first; a **planted wrong-best** fixture must make the oracle **fail loud** (`burned/070`).
- **Ranking-stability under CRN, generalized to K candidates (extends Act-3's 2-arm test).** The *ranking* — not just each pairwise delta — must be stable on the shared draw matrix. Assert all K policy × conversion-grid candidates, within one seed-set, consume the normals **identical path-for-path across the survivor MFJ→single transition**; perturbing only a candidate's conversion amount must not perturb any other candidate's draw consumption. **Pair every CRN absence-assertion with a presence companion** (`burned/027`): assert "the harness ran N paths and at least one path crossed the survivor transition / an ACA cliff / an IRMAA step" so a vacuous zero-path pass cannot masquerade as stability.
- **Grade calibration — "just do it" really is robust, "coin-flip" really is a coin-flip.** The grades are **robust-across-futures ("just do it")** vs **fragile ("coin-flip — here's what it hinges on")**. Calibration asserts: a "just do it" grade is emitted **only** when the winner's lexicographic advantage over the runner-up holds on the **held-out seed-set B** with margin **beyond the ε-band**; "coin-flip" when the held-out advantage falls **within** noise. The oracle hand-supplies one **known-robust** case (a dominant strategy that wins on any futures) and one **known-fragile** case (two near-tied strategies) and asserts the grade matches. **The N=1 cold-read judges the grade's *tone*; this oracle is the only judge of its *correctness*** (R25) — they are different gates and neither substitutes for the other. Because a **single** held-out B is unbiased but **high-variance**, the grade is computed over a specified **minimum B path floor** (enough paths that the margin-vs-tolerance decision is not itself noise) and a **grade-stability check** asserts the grade for a fixed `(model, goal)` does not flip across independent held-out-B draws — a known-borderline case is forced to the conservative ("coin-flip") reading rather than allowed to luck-flip. **The "independent held-out-B draws" are a deterministic B-*family*, not plural persisted seeds:** the one persisted `seedB` indexes a fixed, well-separated SplitMix/hash family `seedB[0..m-1]` of `m` independent hold-out streams (`m` = the minimum-B-path-floor count), all **re-derivable from the single `seedB`** — so the grade is both stability-checked *and* **byte-reproducible on re-entry** (Unit 17 re-solves on the saved `seedB` and regenerates the identical family → the identical grade, never a grade that can't be reproduced). The per-solve compute profile (Unit 15) must budget the `m`-draw family, not one B. **The grade also emits its "what it hinges on" named driver** (consumed by Unit 16, contract #7): `gradeCalibration.ts` runs a small **sensitivity probe** — re-rank the top candidates under the ACA-enhanced-subsidy toggle and a fixture-vintage perturbation — and reports the single input whose flip moves the grade (e.g. "depends on whether enhanced ACA subsidies return"); a pure seed-B near-tie with no identifiable input driver carries a **`sampling-noise-near-tie` sentinel**, never a fabricated input cause. **Grade vs. display resolution are reconciled:** a graded-significant advantage that rounds **below one display tenth** of the `X of 10` headline must not render an *unchanged* `X of 10` under a "just do it" (indistinguishable from no-change) — the surface either shows the finer figure (the surplus `$` delta / a percentile-shift line) or the grade requires the advantage to clear **both** the seed-B ε-band **and** one display tenth before "just do it" (the sub-tenth win otherwise collapses into the no-change state). Seed-set B and the grade thresholds are drawn under a **fresh-draw, used-once-per-release, re-drawn-on-any-change-to-candidates-or-thresholds** protocol; the N=1 cold-read may revise tone/copy but its feedback **never** re-tunes the candidate set, thresholds, or the survival-equivalence tolerance against B-derived outputs (adaptive reuse silently re-optimisms B — the garden-of-forking-paths failure).
- **The held-out-seed defense + the ε-band measurement (contract #2).** `heldOutSeed.ts` runs the candidate evaluation on **seed-set A** (selection) and **seed-set B** (reporting), each CRN-correct internally. **Seed-set B carries the *displayed* numbers, not only the grade:** the recommended figure and the no-action baseline Unit 16 renders both come from B (the `seedA` selection score is never displayed — else the hero number is the curse-biased one). The K-candidate ranking-stability/CRN check (above) therefore runs on **both** A and B, since B now carries the rendered delta as well as the grade + the ε-band. The **survival-equivalent ε** — the Tier-1 tolerance within which candidates are "equivalent" and the Tier-2 goal decides — is **calibrated here against the optimality oracle** (the constant is this unit's deliverable). Store ε with an **out-of-range sentinel default**, never an in-range fallback (`burned/062`): an unset/uncalibrated ε must be *detectably* unset, never a plausible-looking 0.0x that silently overfits. The exact pinned ε value is **deferred** to the §Strand-5/healthcare primary-pinning (contract #2) — it is **directional-until-pinned** because the directional fixtures decide the very near-ties it governs. **`seedA` is the existing Act-2-minted seed; `seedB` is derived by a hash/SplitMix-expansion of `seedA` into a well-separated stream (never `seedA + 1`)** so B is a provably *independent* hold-out, not a near-copy whose mulberry32 stream correlates with A — a seed-input decorrelation only, not a mulberry32 replacement. **The ε resolves into two calibrated quantities (contract #2):** a **display band** measured on B (the rendered interval) and a **selection tie-tolerance** keyed to the variance of the pairwise *difference* under CRN (a pre-specified / theoretical SE, or computed A-side) that alone decides survival-equivalence — the latter is **never** a B-measured level band reused to rank. Both are sentinel-guarded.
- **The harness is the gate Unit 15 reads (contract #1).** Mint an **opaque "oracle-cleared" token** — constructable **only** by this harness on a clean pass — that Unit 15's `solve`-as-recommendation entry point takes as a **required parameter**, so a recommendation that skips validation is a *compile* error, not a runtime discipline check. The token is withheld until **every** check passes on the pinned fixtures: the optimality oracle (all five cases, including the no-change and after-tax-leave-more guards), K-candidate ranking-stability, grade calibration (with the named-driver probe + the deterministic B-family), the held-out-seed defense, **and** the structural pinning clause — every rec-relevant §Strand-5 + healthcare primary pinned (`directionalUntilPinned === false`) and ε calibrated (a still-directional rec-relevant constant or an uncalibrated-ε sentinel withholds the token). This is the structural enforcement of "validation before recommendation," mirroring Act 1's no-unkeyed-write structural seam (the store exposes no write path before a key exists; here the solver exposes no recommendation path before the oracle clears).

**Patterns to follow.** Act 1 seeding/CRN + the externally-derived-golden discipline; `burned/070` (planted-fail self-test — the oracle must be able to fail); `burned/027` (absence-tests need presence companions); `burned/062` (no in-range default fallbacks — the ε sentinel); the "Solver validation" strand of [docs/research/engine-validation-and-tax.md](../research/engine-validation-and-tax.md).

**Test scenarios.**
- Happy path (oracle): the solver's ranking puts the hand-derived known-best drawdown order / bracket-filling conversion optimum **first**; the score margin is reproducible.
- Edge case (cliff-aware oracle): a conversion that fills past an ACA 400%-FPL ceiling or an IRMAA step **ranks below** the cliff-respecting candidate — proving the healthcare overlay flips the ranking, not just the magnitude.
- Edge case (planted wrong-best): a fixture whose declared "best" is actually inferior makes the oracle **fail loud** (`burned/070`) — proving the oracle can detect a wrong recommendation.
- Edge case (K-candidate CRN): all candidates within a seed-set consume normals identical path-for-path across the MFJ→single transition; perturbing one candidate's conversion amount perturbs no other's draws; presence companion confirms ≥1 path crossed each load-bearing boundary (`burned/027`).
- Edge case (grade calibration): the known-robust case grades "just do it"; the known-fragile case grades "coin-flip"; a grade that disagrees with the hand-computed robustness fails the suite.
- Edge case (held-out-seed): selecting on seed-set A and grading on the **same** seed (the curse) yields an over-optimistic grade the held-out-B path corrects — proving B is load-bearing, not decorative.
- Edge case (seed independence): `seedB` derived from `seedA` yields a statistically decorrelated draw matrix; a planted `seedB = seedA + 1` is rejected (a decorrelation check distinct from the U1 period spike).
- Edge case (grade stability): a borderline `(model, goal)` whose grade flips between independent held-out-B draws is detected and forced to the conservative "coin-flip" reading.
- Edge case (ε split): the survival-equivalence set is computed from the selection tie-tolerance (CRN-difference-keyed), never read off the B display band; a level-keyed band wide enough to swallow a CRN-resolvable survival gap (a worse-Tier-1 candidate winning on Tier-2) fails loud.
- Error path (ε sentinel): an uncalibrated ε reads as the out-of-range sentinel and the solver refuses to declare survival-equivalence (fails loud), never silently using an in-range default (`burned/062`).
- Edge case (after-tax leave-more oracle): a fixture where the **gross**-bequest-best strategy is not the **after-tax**-best ranks the after-tax winner first; a solver that argmaxes the gross bequest fails loud (case (iv) — proving the contract-#4 after-tax objective is the one optimized).
- Edge case (no-change oracle): a fixture where conventional drawdown + conversion-0 is the known-best routes to the **no-change state**, never a fabricated active move; the conventional / conversion-0 baseline is asserted present in the candidate set (case (v)).
- Edge case (pick-quality shrinkage): a planted near-tie between two **non-conventional** candidates defaults to the conventional (shrunk) pick unless a candidate's advantage survives the shrinkage; a known-dominant strategy survives shrinkage unchanged.
- Edge case (named driver): a near-tie whose ranking flips under the ACA-enhanced toggle emits that named driver; a pure seed-B near-tie emits the `sampling-noise-near-tie` sentinel, never a fabricated input cause.
- Edge case (B-family reproducibility): the grade computed over the `seedB`-derived B-family is byte-identical when the family is re-derived from the same `seedB` (the re-entry reproducibility Unit 17 relies on).
- Error path (directional-fixture gate): a still-directional rec-relevant constant (`directionalUntilPinned === true`) or an uncalibrated ε **withholds** the oracle-cleared token; the `solve`-as-recommendation entry will not type-check / run (contract #1).
- Integration (the gate): Unit 15's solver entry point is unreachable as a *recommendation* path without the harness-minted oracle-cleared token (contract #1).

**Verification.** The recommendation's correctness is testable before any surface renders it: the oracle ranks the known-best case first and fails loud on a planted wrong-best; the ranking is CRN-stable across K candidates over the survivor transition; grades are calibrated against hand-computed robust/fragile cases; the winner is selected on seed-set A and graded on the held-out seed-set B; the ε-band is calibrated (directional-until-pinned) and sentinel-guarded. The harness guarantees the grade is **honest** (held-out, unbiased for whatever was chosen) and that hand-derivable known-best cases **rank first**. An honest grade alone does **not** make a stochastic near-tie *pick* optimal — a "coin-flip"-graded recommendation can still crown a seed-A-overfit winner over an equally-good or better candidate — so **pick-quality defense is IN scope, not deferred**: Unit 15's `select.ts` applies **selection-stage skeptical/Bayesian shrinkage of the seed-A candidate scores toward the conventional-ordering prior** *before* the argmax, and Unit 14 carries the **calibration case** that proves it — a planted near-tie between two non-conventional candidates must default to the conventional (shrunk) pick **unless** a candidate's advantage survives the shrinkage, and a known-dominant strategy must survive it unchanged. (This closes the gap a no-change-vs-conventional safety net alone leaves open: the curse bites hardest among two *aggressive* near-tied candidates, neither near the conventional baseline.) **No Unit-16 surface may treat a solver output as a recommendation until this harness passes** (contract #1).

**Execution note.** Test-first and **first in the act** — the oracle + ranking-stability + calibration fixtures are authored and passing before any `src/engine/solver/` code exists. This is the literal expression of "validation gates the solver."

## Unit 15 — Solver core

*Named-policies × conversion-grid search on identical CRN draws, lexicographic objective, deterministic selection, the WASM measurement gate.*

**Goal.** Search the bounded strategy space — named drawdown policies × a conversion grid — on identical CRN futures, score each candidate on the lexicographic objective whose metric *is* the headline metric, select a byte-identical winner + retained runner-up, and do it within the on-demand compute budget (or trigger the WASM promotion) — **ranking** candidates, never **re-implementing** decumulation.

**Requirements.** R9 (a strategy over two coupled controls), R21 (lexicographic objective; objective ≡ headline metric), R23 (retain the runner-up), R24 (healthcare couples into the objective). *Gated by Unit 14 (contract #1).*

**Dependencies.** **Unit 14** (the validation gate — contract #1), Act 1 (the spine + the per-year update function + the CRN draw schedule + the sequencing **policy** substrate; the tax-and-accounts overlay; the healthcare overlay — ACA fixed-point, IRMAA 2-yr lag, HSA bucket, the two MAGI calculators), Act 3 (the budget — essentials = the Tier-1 floor, discretionary = the Tier-2 surplus; the manual-control `roth.ts` per-year update shape), Act 2 (`memoryModel` — the solver run is dispatched through the same orchestrator + request-epoch as the spine).

**Files.**
- Create: `src/engine/solver/search.ts` (the policy × conversion-grid cross-product evaluator, on identical CRN draws — **reuses the spine's per-year update function**, does not re-implement decumulation), `src/engine/solver/objective.ts` (the lexicographic Tier-1/Tier-2 score), `src/engine/solver/select.ts` (deterministic byte-identical selection + the retained runner-up — contract #3), `src/engine/solver/cancel.ts` (request-epoch cooperative cancellation), `src/engine/solver/profile.ts` (the WASM measurement harness — the compute-profile gate)
- Modify: `src/shared/model.ts` (the **chosen-goal** field — the Tier-2 pick — under the Act-4 `schemaVersion`-3 bump; the saved-recommendation record is Unit 17's), `src/store/memoryModel.ts` (dispatch the on-demand solve + carry its request-epoch, extending the Act-2 worker handle), `src/engine/engine.worker.ts` (expose the solve entry behind the Unit-14 oracle-cleared gate)
- Test: `src/engine/solver/__tests__/search.test.ts`, `objective.test.ts`, `select.test.ts`, `cancel.test.ts`, `profile.test.ts`

**Approach.**
- **The search space is bounded and named (R-locked, see [docs/product.md §4 D4](../product.md)):** the **named drawdown policies** `{proportional, taxable-first, pre-tax-first, bracket-fill}` (the Act-1 sequencing substrate) × a **conversion grid `{amount × years}`**. The solver evaluates the **cross-product** of candidates; the grid resolution (amount step, max years) is a bounded set — exact resolution **deferred to implementation** (tuned against the compute budget below and the legibility of the comparative story). This is **not** a continuous optimizer (a full continuous optimizer + a "die-with-zero" solver + SS-claiming-age are explicitly chapter-two, deferred). **The grid is cliff-anchored, not uniform — a correctness requirement, not a resolution-tuning knob:** the ACA 400%-FPL cliff and the IRMAA steps are discontinuities whose optimum sits exactly at the just-under-threshold dollar, so each candidate year includes grid points **pinned just under every active ACA/IRMAA threshold plus a local bisection refinement around them**; a uniform grid generically steps over that point and can neither find the just-under-cliff optimum nor avoid recommending a cliff-tripping amount. Relatedly, **`bracket-fill` fills to the *binding effective ceiling* for the year** — the subsidy-aware MAGI line in ACA years, the next IRMAA step post-65, a marginal-rate-aware bracket edge otherwise — **never the nominal tax-bracket top** (which overshoots the cliff the instant healthcare is on). The candidate generation is the **shared `candidates.ts`** (authored in Unit 14, so the harness and the search score the identical set — never two drifting enumerations).
- **The "no-action baseline" is a labeled, scored candidate — the user's CURRENT strategy, not a notional default (contract #6 anchor).** Every Unit-16 comparison (the no-change decision, the rendered delta, the tax-aware reframe) is *"current strategy → recommended,"* so the baseline must be a real candidate scored on the **identical seed-A and seed-B draws** as every other. The search **forces the user's current configured strategy into the candidate set as a labeled baseline**: their current sequencing policy + current conversion, defaulting to `proportional` / conversion-0 if none is set. A returning Act-3 user who set a **custom per-bucket drawdown order** (not one of the four named policies) is handled explicitly — that custom order is evaluated as its own labeled baseline point **outside** the named-policy × grid cross-product (scored on the same draws), so the delta is never silently anchored against the conventional default the user does not actually hold. The conventional-order / conversion-0 candidate is *also* always present (the Unit-14 no-change oracle case (v) and the shrinkage prior both require it).
- **Ranks, does not re-decumulate (the solver-on-top principle).** Each candidate is evaluated by running the **existing** spine + overlays through the **same per-year update function** the manual controls drive (Act-3's `roth.ts` shape) with that candidate's policy + conversion event — so order-of-operations can never drift between the spine, the manual controls, and the solver. **The reduce-to-spine golden invariant is inherited, not re-proven:** with conversion = 0 and the conventional drawdown, a candidate's distribution is byte-identical (same seed) to the validated Trinity/Bengen spine — the golden cases are never perturbed by the solver layer (see [docs/architecture.md §5](../architecture.md)).
- **Identical CRN draws across all candidates (contract #5).** Every candidate, within a seed-set, consumes the **one** market-return draw per year, identical path-for-path — so the ranking is signal, not luck, and asset-location is structurally foreclosed. The K-candidate CRN test (authored in Unit 14) is the proof. The death-order conditional filter carries through unchanged (same seed, same draws, no re-draw). `seedA` is the existing Act-2 seed and `seedB` is its hash/SplitMix-expansion into a well-separated stream (Unit 14) — independent, never `seedA + 1`.
- **The lexicographic objective ≡ the headline metric (contract #4; R21).** **Tier 1** = the survival-floor statistic — essentials covered every year across futures, in the spine's **`X of 10`** metric (the budget's essentials lines, Act 3, are the floor). Candidates within the **selection tie-tolerance** (Unit 14 / contract #2 — the CRN-difference-keyed tolerance computed at selection, **never** the B-measured display band; using B to choose re-contaminates the held-out) are **survival-equivalent**; seed-set B renders the displayed survival number, it does not pick the equivalent set. **Tier 2** = the user's chosen surplus goal, each a **concrete distributional statistic** `objective.ts` computes in the same units the headline pivot renders (contract #4): *pay less tax* = lifetime tax paid; *leave more* = the **after-tax-to-heirs** bequest (the first-order §1014/IRD adjustment at the disclosed assumed heir bracket — **never** the gross figure, which can crown the after-tax loser, contract #7); *live bigger now* = the held-out **`X of 10` confidence that the user's chosen front-loaded discretionary shape holds** (a confidence-over-fixed-spending statistic — the solver never raises a spend amount; contract #8a). The objective's quantity **is** the headline's quantity — there is no separate internal score the headline could contradict.
- **Deterministic byte-identical selection (contract #3), with pick-quality shrinkage.** Argmax over many candidates on seed-set A overfits A's noise (the optimizer's curse), so `select.ts` first applies a **deterministic skeptical/Bayesian shrinkage of the seed-A candidate scores toward the conventional-ordering prior** (the Unit-14-calibrated lever for *pick* quality, not just grade honesty) — a candidate only displaces the conventional pick if its advantage **survives** the shrinkage; a known-dominant strategy survives it unchanged. Selection then = quantized lexicographic objective over the shrunk scores + a fixed lexicographic tie-break (policy enum order → conversion amount → years) + a seeded sub-stream **orthogonal** to seed-sets A/B (used only for a residual deterministic tie-break, never for simulation). The winner + the **retained runner-up** (R23 — the runner-up is kept, not discarded, so Unit 16 can show "why this beat it") are reproducible from `(model, seedA, seedB, goal)` alone. No clock/entropy/`Math.random` (Act 1's engine-purity lint enforces it).
- **The 10/10-clamp / surplus regime (feeds contract #6).** When Tier-1 is maxed across futures (survival a given), the objective's *active discriminator* is Tier-2 — the solver emits the surplus winner and flags the regime so Unit 16 can pivot the headline. The clamp is on the *display* of Tier-1; the **selection** still uses the raw pre-clamp Tier-1 to decide survival-equivalence (mirroring Act-3's raw-pre-clamp monotonicity rule). **The surplus-regime flag requires A and B to AGREE, within an over-funded ε** — and "over-funded" is an **ε-tolerance, not an exact 10/10**: the flag trips when the B-rendered Tier-1 is within the display-band ε of the ceiling, so a genuinely over-funded household whose B happens to miss one path by a rounding step is not permanently denied the pivot it most needs. Both disagreement directions are pinned: **(a)** Tier-1 maxed on A (selection) but a real survival gap on B (beyond the over-funded ε) → the flag does **not** trip (no false "safe either way" the displayed B number would contradict); **(b)** B is safe-either-way but the *winner was selected on A's Tier-1 discriminator* (the candidates were not Tier-1-equivalent on A) → the flag does **not** trip either, because pivoting the headline to "survival is a given" while the pick was made on a survival edge would diverge the objective from the headline (contract #4). The pivot fires **only** when both A and B agree the floor is non-binding (the candidates are survival-equivalent on the A-side selection tolerance *and* B is within the over-funded ε of the ceiling); otherwise the survival headline stands.
- **Spending is never a control (contract #8a).** The budget + spending shape (go-go-years front-loading vs level) are **read-only input** to every candidate; the solver moves only sequencing + conversion. No candidate alters a budget line.
- **Candidate-legality filter before scoring (validate-before-mutate, `burned/021`).** Before a `{policy, amount, years}` candidate is scored, each year is checked for legality: past RMD age the year's **RMD is a forced ordinary-income floor taken first and is non-convertible**, so the conversion is clamped to the pre-tax balance remaining **after** the RMD, and a grid amount that exceeds post-RMD convertible headroom (or that would convert in lieu of the RMD) is **rejected as infeasible** (an out-of-range sentinel, never silently scored). The search-space generation owns this — Act 1 enforces the RMD when *running* a candidate, but an infeasible candidate must never reach scoring in the first place.
- **Per-account buckets are a hard precondition of the recommendation (the budget-only-path guard).** The solver searches *sequencing × conversion*, which require per-person **pre-tax / Roth / taxable / HSA** buckets — the second beat **cannot be emitted without them**. Buckets always arrive from the account-level intake (Act 2 / R35), so the absent-buckets case is a **defensive edge** — a legacy v1 vault or a direct path. The recommendation-invite **triggers the `RothAccounts` mini-intake when buckets are absent** (reusing the Act-3 component), and the solver **never** runs on a defaulted bucket split (`burned/062`: a defaulted split is a silent measurement, the calm-but-wrong shape).
- **A chosen Tier-2 goal is an equally hard precondition (the no-discriminator guard).** The lexicographic objective needs a Tier-2 goal to break survival-equivalent ties, so the solve is **never dispatched while `goal === unset-sentinel`**, mirroring the bucket guard. The second beat presents the **`GoalPicker` first** (Unit 16) — "what matters most to you above safety?" — and only dispatches the solve once a goal is set; it never runs a Tier-1-only solve and crowns the deterministic tie-break (policy enum order) as if it were advice. This also closes the **over-funded-with-no-goal** case: for a Tier-1-maxed household an unset goal would leave the solver with no discriminator and nothing to pivot the headline to, so the surplus pivot (contract #6) is likewise unreachable until a goal exists — the goal prompt *is* the first interaction, not a wall after a fabricated recommendation.
- **Solve-once-on-demand + cooperative cancellation (request-epoch).** The interaction is **solve-once-on-demand**, not live-drag: a recommendation is computed when invited (the second beat) or re-invited (a goal re-pick / tune). `cancel.ts` extends the Act-2 request-epoch + Act-3 generation-counter discipline to the **solve**: each solve carries the epoch it was dispatched for and **cooperatively cancels** if a newer request supersedes it (checked between candidates / between seed-sets), so a fast goal re-pick never commits a stale recommendation. **The solve has an explicit calm pending state** (mirroring the spine's pending discipline): while a solve runs, the second-beat surface shows a quiet "working on it…" state, the prior recommendation (if any) is held visibly-stale-but-labeled rather than blanked, and **no partial or fabricated recommendation paints** before the solve commits; the committed result replaces pending atomically (notify-once). A multi-second solve on a mid-tier phone must read as "the tool is working," never as "the tool broke" — the pending copy is an Act-4 cold-read subject.
- **The WASM measurement gate (the decided trigger, the port deferred).** `profile.ts` measures the real on-demand solve cost: **named-policies × conversion-grid × 1k paths × (SS fixed-point + ACA fixed-point + IRMAA lag), across BOTH seed-sets A and B**, on a **mid-tier phone**. If the on-demand solve **exceeds the budget**, **WASM moves from fast-follow to LOAD-BEARING** — the trigger is *measured, not guessed*; the WASM port itself is deferred (the TS baseline ships, the measurement decides the promotion; see the WASM CSP landmine in [docs/architecture.md §9–§10](../architecture.md)). The budget threshold value + the mid-tier reference device are **deferred to implementation** (instrument first, per the eye-in-loop discipline). Note for the profile: select-on-A + grade-on-B roughly **doubles** the per-candidate evaluation vs a single-seed solve — the profile must measure both, not extrapolate from one. **The profile must run a deliberately compute-worst-case scenario — longest horizon, largest cliff-anchored grid (the most active ACA/IRMAA thresholds → the most pinned points + bisection refinements), both the pre-65 ACA and post-65 IRMAA regimes exercised — not a median one**, or the WASM promotion is decided on a cost a real near-cliff, long-horizon, large-pre-tax household will blow past (the SS/ACA fixed-points converge in 2–3 bounded passes, so the worst case is driven by horizon + cliff-count/grid-size, not iteration depth). The profile budgets **both** seed-sets **and** the `m`-draw held-out B-family (Unit 14), not a single B. **A bounded in-MVP fallback ladder exists that does NOT depend on the deferred WASM port** (since both solver axes ship co-equal, the worst case is real): (a) a **coarse-then-refine two-stage search** — a cheap coarse grid prunes the field, and the cliff-anchored just-under-threshold points + bisection are spent **only around the surviving candidates** (the cliff anchoring stays a correctness requirement, but it is applied to survivors, not the full cross-product, so the un-cuttable points don't multiply across every coarse candidate); (b) a stated **candidate-count ceiling** for the interactive solve; (c) if the measured budget is still blown **and** WASM is not yet built, the recommendation **degrades honestly rather than blocking** — the calm pending state with cooperative-cancel runs the full-fidelity solve, or a reduced interactive-path solve is shown explicitly labeled with a "full-precision" confirm — it never silently down-samples below the path floor the held-out grade needs. The MVP **ships** (degraded-but-honest), it does not block on the WASM port.
- **The K-candidate solve-result wire-shape (extends Act 1's worker transfer contract).** Act 1 pins the single-run worker result (typed-array buffers via `Comlink.transfer`, detached on the worker side, fresh per run — see [docs/architecture.md §11](../architecture.md)); the solve returns **K candidates**, so the wire shape is made explicit rather than left to the implementer: the solve returns **one structured payload** carrying the **full seed-B distributions** for the figures Unit 16 renders — the **winner, the retained runner-up, and the no-action baseline** — plus the grade + named-driver + surplus-regime flag, with **only those distributions' buffers in the transfer-list**; the pruned/losing candidates return **scalar lexicographic scores only** (structured-clone, no buffers), never their full distributions. The per-solve allocation is sized for this bounded set (three distributions + scalars), not K full distributions — keeping Act 1's "fresh per run, retains none" discipline true at K-candidate scale.

**Patterns to follow.** Act 1 CRN + the reduce-to-spine invariant; Act-3 `roth.ts` (reuse the per-year update function, never re-implement decumulation; raw-pre-clamp selection); the Act-2 request-epoch / Act-3 generation counters for cooperative cancellation (`burned/005`); `burned/062` (no in-range default — the goal field has an explicit "unset" sentinel, never a silent default goal); `burned/021` (validate-before-mutate — the candidate-legality / RMD-first filter); the §Strand 4/5 + healthcare research.

**Test scenarios.**
- Golden invariant (inherited): a candidate with conversion = 0 + the conventional drawdown is **byte-identical (same seed) to the spine distribution** — the solver layer introduces no spurious delta (the reduce-to-spine anchor).
- Happy path (search): the cross-product of `{proportional, taxable-first, pre-tax-first, bracket-fill}` × the conversion grid is evaluated; a winner + a retained runner-up are returned.
- Edge case (K-candidate CRN): all candidates consume identical normals path-for-path across the MFJ→single transition; a perturbed conversion amount changes one candidate's score without changing any other's draw consumption (contract #5).
- Edge case (lexicographic): two candidates within the **A-side selection tie-tolerance** (the CRN-difference-keyed tolerance, never the B display band — contract #2) are survival-equivalent → the **Tier-2 goal** decides; a candidate with a higher Tier-2 score but a worse Tier-1 (outside the selection tolerance) **never** wins (the floor is absolute — R21); a planted survival-equivalence decided on a seed-B level band fails loud (held-out re-contamination).
- Edge case (objective ≡ headline): the quantity the solver maximizes is the same quantity Unit 16 renders as the headline — a planted divergence (an internal score ≠ the displayed metric) fails loud.
- Edge case (deterministic selection): repeated solves of an unchanged `(model, seedA, seedB, goal)` return a **byte-identical** winner + grade + runner-up; the tie-break is stable, no `Math.random` reachable.
- Edge case (surplus regime): a Tier-1-maxed (over-funded) household → the solver discriminates on Tier-2 and flags the surplus regime for the headline pivot; the raw pre-clamp Tier-1 still governs survival-equivalence; the flag trips only when **A and B agree** within the over-funded ε — a household Tier-1-maxed on A but showing a survival gap on B beyond ε does **not** trip it (no false "safe either way"), and conversely a B-safe household whose winner was *selected on an A-side Tier-1 edge* (candidates not survival-equivalent on A) does **not** trip it either (objective≡headline); a genuinely over-funded household missing the ceiling by a sub-ε rounding step **does** still pivot.
- Edge case (no-action baseline): the user's current configured strategy is present in the candidate set as a labeled baseline scored on the identical seed-A/seed-B draws; a returning user's **custom per-bucket drawdown order** is evaluated as its own baseline point outside the named-policy × grid cross-product; the rendered delta is **current→recommended**, never conventional-default→recommended; the conventional / conversion-0 candidate is also always present.
- Edge case (pick-quality shrinkage): a near-tie between two **non-conventional** candidates resolves to the conventional (shrunk) pick unless an advantage survives shrinkage; a known-dominant strategy is unaffected; the shrunk selection is byte-reproducible.
- Edge case (goal precondition): a solve dispatched while `goal === unset-sentinel` is **refused** — the `GoalPicker` is presented first and no Tier-1-only tie-break is crowned/graded as advice; an over-funded household with no goal sees the goal prompt, not a fabricated surplus pivot.
- Edge case (spending immutable): no candidate alters a budget line; the budget is read-only across the search (contract #8a).
- Edge case (compute fallback): when the worst-case profile exceeds the budget and WASM is not built, the coarse-then-refine + candidate-ceiling fallback keeps the solve within budget at full path-floor fidelity (cliff anchoring applied around survivors), or it degrades to an explicitly-labeled reduced-path interactive solve with a full-precision confirm — never a silent down-sample below the held-out path floor.
- Edge case (cliff-anchored grid): a grid that omits the just-under-cliff point fails to find the cliff-optimal candidate the Unit-14 oracle declares best — proving the grid, not just the ranking, is cliff-aware.
- Edge case (candidate legality): a grid amount exceeding post-RMD convertible headroom in an RMD-active year is rejected as infeasible and never selected; the conversion never substitutes for the RMD.
- Edge case (cancellation): a goal re-pick mid-solve cooperatively cancels the in-flight solve via request-epoch; only the latest solve's recommendation is committed (no stale recommendation paints).
- Integration (the gate): the solve entry refuses to emit a *recommendation* without Unit 14's harness-minted oracle-cleared token (contract #1).
- Integration (compute profile): `profile.ts` measures named-policies × grid × 1k paths × (SS + ACA + IRMAA) across **both** seed-sets on the reference device and reports whether the on-demand budget is exceeded (the WASM trigger).

**Verification.** The solver ranks the bounded policy × conversion-grid space on identical CRN futures, selects a byte-identical winner + retained runner-up under a lexicographic objective whose metric is the headline metric, reduces byte-identically to the validated spine when off, never touches spending, cancels cooperatively on a re-pick, and reports a measured (compute-worst-case) profile that decides the WASM promotion — its conversion grid is **cliff-anchored** (contains the just-under-cliff optimum), every candidate clears the **RMD-first legality filter**, and the survival-equivalent set is decided by the CRN-difference selection tolerance (never the B display band) — and it does none of this as a *recommendation* until Unit 14's harness passes.

## Unit 16 — Recommendation surface

*Recommend-second, confidence-graded, comparative, hedge-on-headline, the surplus pivot.*

**Goal.** Surface the solver's output as the calm **second beat**: a confidence-graded recommendation that never contradicts the spine, with the hedge on the headline, the runner-up retained and the comparison one tap down, the modeled omissions disclosed adjacent to the delta, every input + reasoning visible/editable, and an honest 10/10→surplus pivot — landing as *earned*, never overconfident.

**Requirements.** R10 (recommend-second flow), R4 (detail on demand — the comparison/reasoning one tap down), R11 (calm, invited, never a nagging alert), R12 (probabilistic framing; certainty banned), R13 (optional honest-limits note), R14 (plain not dumbed-down), R7 (every recommendation input + reasoning visible/editable — gains weight here), R21 (the headline pivot), R22 (grade its own confidence; hedge on the headline), R23 (comparative; runner-up retained), R24 (healthcare seen, not silently optimized over).

**Dependencies.** **Unit 15** (the solver output — winner, grade, retained runner-up, surplus-regime flag), Act 2 (the spine confidence statement the second beat follows + the copyGuard catalog + the outcome-state set; the colorblind-safe viz primitives + the `X of 10` display contract — built under the project's `back-nine-design` law: color is never the only signal), Act 3 (the budget two-tier essentials/lifestyle reading; the `require-the-hedge` lint + the manual-control surfaces the goal re-pick reuses; the sharpen loop the recommendation inputs become editable through).

**Files.**
- Create: `src/ui/Recommendation.tsx` (the second-beat surface), `src/ui/ConfidenceGrade.tsx` ("just do it" vs "coin-flip — here's what it hinges on"), `src/ui/RunnerUp.tsx` (the retained runner-up + "why this beat it"), `src/ui/GoalPicker.tsx` (the Tier-2 pick — leave more / pay less tax / live bigger now), `src/viz/RecommendationViz.tsx` (the recommended-vs-current two-series, **string-free** — text via props from `copy.ts`, reusing Act-2's encoding tokens)

- Test: `src/ui/__tests__/recommendation.test.tsx`, `confidenceGrade.test.tsx`, `runnerUp.test.tsx`, `goalPicker.test.tsx`

**Approach.**
- **Recommend-second, never recommend-first (contract #6; R10).** The spine confidence statement is the **first** beat and is **never overwritten** (Act-1 seed/headline identity). The recommendation renders as the immediate **second** beat — *"and here's what we'd do about it"* — only after the spine answer exists; the comparative reasoning (the runner-up + "why this beat it") lives **one tap down** (R4 depth-on-demand), never unsolicited on the first frame. It is **calm and invited** — no badge, no alert, no engagement bait (R11). **The invited affordance is a real, named control** (its exact pixels/copy are a cold-read deliverable, but its *existence and shape* are decided here so an implementer never invents it): a single calm affordance rendered just below the spine confidence statement that the user activates to open the second beat — never an auto-trigger on scroll/load (which would violate R11's "invited") and never a directive "Get our recommendation" CTA (which would violate R12's banned certainty/imperative voice). Activating it opens the `GoalPicker` as the **first** interaction (the goal precedes the solve, Unit 15), so the second beat *begins* with "what matters most to you above safety?" rather than a fabricated ranked answer.
- **The no-change recommendation state (R25 — the recommender's calm-but-wrong shape).** When the winner is **within the A-side selection tolerance of the no-action baseline** — the conventional order is *frequently* already optimal (it is literally Unit-14 oracle case (i)/(v)) — the second beat does **not** manufacture an active move: it renders a **no-change state** (reusing Act-2's outcome-state vocabulary) — honestly *"you're already on the best path we found; here's the close runner-up if you're curious"* — wearing its hedge, the runner-up still one tap down. **No-change is a SELECTION decision (it picks *what to recommend*), so it is decided on the CRN-difference selection tolerance computed at selection (contract #2), NEVER on a seed-B level band** — using B to decide no-change-vs-active re-contaminates the held-out exactly as using B to rank would; seed-B then supplies only the *displayed* numbers and the grade for the no-change verdict. The no-change state **carries its own calibrated grade** (Unit 14) — "just do it" when the baseline dominates across the held-out futures, "coin-flip" when the margin to the runner-up is within noise — and its headline **wears the hedge** by construction (the `require-the-hedge` lint covers the no-change copy; "you're already on the best path we found" is a hedged claim, not a certainty). It is a named Act-4 cold-read subject. Fabricating an active recommendation when no change beats the status quo is the recommender's cardinal-sin shape. **Precedence with the surplus pivot:** when the surplus regime is active **and** the winner is within the selection tolerance of the baseline on Tier-2, the two states **compose** — render *"you're safe either way, and you're already on the best surplus path we found"*, **never** the active "keeps ~$X more" surplus claim (which would fabricate a surplus gain that does not exist).
- **Confidence grading on the headline (contract #7; R22).** The grade ("just do it" / "coin-flip — here's what it hinges on") rides the **headline**, never buried in tapped-away math. The grade is the **calibrated** grade from Unit 14's held-out-seed reporting — the surface renders it, it does not recompute robustness. The "coin-flip" render names *what it hinges on* (the near-tie's sensitive input — e.g. "this depends on whether the enhanced ACA subsidies return"), turning fragility into honesty rather than wishy-washiness. Depth-on-demand must never invert into certainty-on-top / caveats-below. The *"what it hinges on"* line requires a **sensitive-input attribution distinct from the seed-B margin grade** — a seed-B dispersion cannot name *which input* a ranking hinges on, and a legislative/scenario sensitivity (the ACA-enhanced toggle) is invisible to it — so **Unit 14's `gradeCalibration` sensitivity probe emits the named driver** (re-ranking the top candidates under the ACA-enhanced toggle + a fixture-vintage perturbation, reporting the single input whose flip moves the grade) alongside the grade; the surface only renders it, and a pure seed-B near-tie carries the `sampling-noise-near-tie` sentinel — a *sampling*-framed hinge, never a fabricated input cause.
- **Objective ≡ headline, rendered explicitly (contract #4).** The recommendation's headline metric **is** the metric the solver optimized — rendered in the spine's **`X of 10`** voice ("this pushes the survivor's essentials from 7 of 10 to 9 of 10") so it can never contradict the spine answer. **Every displayed figure — the recommended-strategy figure, the no-action baseline it is compared against, AND the retained runner-up's figure — is rendered from the held-out seed-set B** (contract #2): the shown shift is therefore unbiased (never the `seedA` *selection* score, which is the optimizer's-curse-inflated figure the whole Unit-14 machinery exists to defeat) and internally CRN-consistent (every number on the one `seedB` draw matrix → both the recommended-vs-baseline and the recommended-vs-runner-up deltas are **pure strategy signal**, not an A-vs-B sampling artifact). The `seedA` score selects the winner (and retains the runner-up) only; it is **never rendered** — for either the winner or the runner-up. **The 10/10→surplus pivot (contract #6; R21):** when Unit 15 flags the surplus regime (survival a given), the headline honestly **switches** to the chosen surplus metric — *"you're safe either way; this keeps ~$X more from the IRS / funds ~N more years of travel"* — with the active metric **named** so the user never silently re-bases between beats. The pivot reuses the spine's over-funded outcome-state vocabulary.
- **Tax-blind→tax-aware reframe, inherited from Act 3 (contract #6; R8/R10).** The Act-2 spine headline (the first beat) runs the tax + healthcare overlays **OFF** — it is tax-blind, on `seedA` — and is **never overwritten** (Act-1 seed/headline identity). The recommendation's baseline + recommended numbers are the **tax-aware** picture (overlays ON, on `seedB`), so the no-action baseline can legitimately read **different from** the tax-blind spine number the user anchored on. Inherit Act-3's **delta-led reframe** — *"now that we know your account types, this is the tax-aware picture"* — so the shift reads as a *sharpening to a truer picture*, **never a silent downgrade** of the magic-moment number; the **active baseline is named** so the differentiation "move" (the delta — both numbers tax-aware, both on `seedB`) reads against a consistent anchor, not against the tax-blind spine figure. The magnitude the copy attributes to taxes is **only** the `seedB` overlays-off→on delta (the strategy/tax signal on one draw matrix); the baseline-vs-spine difference is framed qualitatively as *"a sharper picture now that account types are known,"* **never quantified as a tax-caused drop** — the residual seedA↔seedB difference is sampling, not taxes, and is never shown as a number the user could misread as a downgrade. *(The picture is account-aware from the account-level intake onward, so the baseline-vs-spine difference is a sharpening to a truer picture, never a tax-caused drop. The delta-led honesty rule, the never-overwrite-the-saved-headline rule, and the never-quantify-the-seedA↔seedB-residual rule all stand.)* This is the surface half of the precondition Unit 15 enforces: the second beat is only reachable once per-account buckets exist, which is the moment the picture turns tax-aware.
- **Comparative transparency, runner-up retained (R23).** `RunnerUp.tsx` keeps and surfaces the runner-up (Unit 15 retained it) — "why this beat the runner-up," not a formula dump. Rendered via `RecommendationViz` as a two-series recommended-vs-runner-up (or recommended-vs-current) using Act-2's colorblind-safe encoding tokens + the delta-as-hero treatment (Act-3's `TwoFutures` lineage); the N=1 reader is color blind, so the delta carries a non-color hero signal (the project's `back-nine-design` law — color is never the only signal).
- **Disclosed omissions adjacent to the delta (contract #7; R24).** ACA-PTC + IRMAA are already **IN** the model (Act 1) precisely because a disclosed omission can **invert** a ranking. The remaining **out-but-disclosed** effects — **NIIT, state tax** — are disclosed in plain language **next to the delta**, never silently dropped; **when the chosen goal is *leave more*, the bequest is the *after-tax-to-heirs* figure (a first-order §1014/IRD adjustment modeled INTO the objective, contract #4/#7 — never the gross figure), and the *assumed heir tax bracket* is the disclosed, R7-editable assumption surfaced adjacent to the leave-more delta** (the lever-sensitive effect is modeled, not omitted; only the lever-inert NIIT/state remain disclosed-and-omitted); a conversion that **trips an ACA cliff or an IRMAA step is shown, not silently optimized over** (the healthcare-blind delta is sign-inverted). **The held-fixed SS claim age is disclosed adjacent to the delta** — it is not a solver-optimized control in MVP (chapter-two), yet changing it would move provisional-income taxation / ACA-MAGI / IRMAA-MAGI and could invert the ranking, so a calm note ("this assumes your current claim ages") rides the delta rather than the assumption being silently buried (the same disclosed-could-invert discipline as §1014). **For the *live bigger now* / *leave more* goals, the delta funds the user's CURRENT chosen spending shape** (a user-set input, contract #8a) — a one-line honesty note says the recommendation optimizes the *funding* of the shape you set, it does not endorse or propose a lifestyle, so a surplus headline is never read as the solver telling you how to live. **When a recommended strategy's delta leans on the ACA effect, the SLCSP-benchmark-level and CSR caveats (Act 3 — the SLCSP is the user-input the entire ACA fixed-point keys on) are surfaced adjacent to the rec delta too** (at least by reference to the Act-3 healthcare surface), so an ACA-affected recommendation is never presented more certain than its inputs — especially when a **fallback (non-real) SLCSP** is in use.
- **Every input + reasoning visible/editable; the user stays pilot (R7, R10d).** R7 gains weight under recommend-second: the recommendation exposes its inputs **and its reasoning** for the user to trust and approve. The user **tunes / overrides / re-picks the goal** via `GoalPicker` (reusing the Act-3 sharpen loop + manual-control surfaces); on a re-pick, the solve re-runs (Unit 15, request-epoch) and **both futures update**. A `GoalPicker` with no goal yet selected uses an **explicit unset sentinel** (`burned/062`), never a silent default goal — the recommendation does not assert a goal the user never picked.
- **Saving a recommendation is an explicit committed gesture, never inferred (the bridge to Unit 17's "executed action").** A solved recommendation lives in `memoryModel` as an **un-saved hypothetical** (Unit 15) until the user **deliberately commits it** — an explicit, calm "save this recommendation" gesture (the affordance's pixels deferred to the cold-read; its *existence* decided here). The tool **never** auto-saves on solve, on close, or on a goal re-pick — because Unit 17 treats a saved recommendation as a **real, executed action** with serious staleness consequences ("the action we recommended may no longer be advised"), and that severity is only honest if the user consciously committed. The un-saved hypothetical can be tuned, re-picked, and abandoned freely with no staleness obligation; only the explicit commit mints the saved-recommendation record (Unit 17's `schemaVersion`-3 write).
- **The `require-the-hedge` lint extended to the recommendation (contract #7; R12).** All recommendation strings route through the `copy.ts` catalog; `RecommendationViz` (in `src/viz`) stays **string-free**, receiving text as props (`burned/063`), so the catalog-enumeration covers the most sensitive strings by construction. copyGuard's **`require-the-hedge`** lint (the positive/require assertion from Act 3) is extended to assert the **recommendation headline + the grade copy** each carry a probabilistic hedge; certainty verbs ("guaranteed / optimal / locks in / you will save") stay banned. **The "just do it" collision is resolved explicitly:** require-the-hedge applies to the rendered grade **sentence/copy** (which must wear its hedge — e.g. *"across the futures we tested, this holds up — just do it"*), and **exempts the bare fixed enum label** ("just do it" / "coin-flip") as an internal identifier — so the punchy robust label survives while the user-visible grade copy still hedges by construction. **New recommendation slots** added to the allowlist: the recommended-strategy descriptor (policy name + conversion amount/years as user-echo, not a product claim), the grade label, the `~$X` surplus delta, and the `X of 10` shift — added to the catalog-enumeration test so they pass by construction.
- **The optional honest-limits note (R13).** The *"this is a model — validate big, irreversible moves with a pro"* note is available adjacent to the recommendation on **honesty** grounds (not a Terms requirement) — calm and invited, not a nagging gate; it lives outside the `require-the-hedge` input the same way the static disclaimer is surface-scoped.

**Patterns to follow.** Act-2 copyGuard + the outcome-state set + the `X of 10` display contract; Act-3 `TwoFutures` delta-as-hero + the props-from-`copy.ts` string-free viz rule; Act-3 `require-the-hedge` lint; `emil-design-eng` (calm second-beat entrance, no count-up on the surplus dollar); `frontend-design:frontend-design` (filtered through back-nine-design — the recommendation reads as a calm co-pilot, not a dashboard); `back-nine-design` (project design law — color is never the only signal).

- Edge case (surplus pivot): an over-funded household renders the surplus metric as the headline ("safe either way; keeps ~$X more from the IRS") with the active metric named — not a fake `X of 10` survival difference.
- Edge case (runner-up retained): the runner-up is present and "why this beat it" is reachable one tap down; stripping the runner-up fails the suite (R23).
- Edge case (omission disclosure): NIIT + state tax are disclosed adjacent to the delta; a conversion that trips an ACA cliff / IRMAA step is shown, not hidden (R24); a *leave more* goal renders the **after-tax-to-heirs** bequest (the §1014/IRD adjustment modeled in) with the **assumed heir bracket disclosed adjacent** to the delta — a planted **gross**-bequest objective, or a missing heir-bracket disclosure, fails the suite.
- Edge case (goal re-pick): re-picking the Tier-2 goal re-solves (request-epoch) and updates both futures; an unset goal uses the explicit sentinel, never a silent default.
- Error path (copyGuard): the catalog-enumeration asserts every recommendation string passes; `require-the-hedge` rejects an un-hedged headline/grade; the **robust** grade's *rendered copy* passes require-the-hedge (not only the coin-flip copy), while the bare enum label is exempt; a certainty verb is rejected; `RecommendationViz` is proven string-free (all text props-from-catalog).
- Edge case (color-independence): the recommended-vs-runner-up delta is legible in grayscale (the delta-as-hero non-color signal); the N=1 reader is color blind.
- Integration (visibly moves the answer): a recommendation **visibly moves** the spine answer ("6 of 10 → 8 of 10") **and** is correctly confidence-graded (the differentiation success criterion).

**Verification.** The recommendation lands as a calm, invited second beat that never contradicts the spine, wears its calibrated hedge on the headline, retains and explains the runner-up, discloses NIIT/state adjacent to the delta and never hides an ACA/IRMAA cliff, pivots honestly to the surplus metric when survival is a given, exposes every input + reasoning as editable, and passes copyGuard's `require-the-hedge` by construction. **Passing the N=1 cold-read on the recommendation — earned (not overconfident), the "coin-flip" grade honest (not wishy-washy or alarming), the comparison reading as transparency (not a bait-and-switch) — is an Act-4 exit condition** (R25; the cold-read judges *tone*, Unit 14 judges *correctness*).

## Unit 17 — Stale saved-recommendation handling

*A saved rec is an executed action, so it re-solves under current fixtures.*

**Goal.** Treat a saved recommendation as the real, executed action it is: on re-entry, **re-solve it under current fixtures** (never re-present a stale strategy as still-advised), say *"the action we recommended may no longer be advised"* (not *"your number drifted"*), and re-verify the ACA legislative status that can flip the whole pre-65 model.

**Requirements.** R7 (the saved recommendation's inputs/reasoning re-surface), R10 (the recommendation re-presents honestly on return), R24 (ACA legislative volatility re-verified), R25 (a stale recommendation acted on is the cardinal-sin shape — calm-but-wrong over time).

**Dependencies.** **Unit 15** (the re-solve path), **Unit 16** (the re-presented recommendation surface + grade render), Act 3 (the per-surface staleness map + the returning-user re-entry this **inverts** for a recommendation), Act 1 (the encrypted store + the saved-recommendation record's persistence — see [docs/architecture.md §7.3](../architecture.md)), `src/engine/constants/` (the year-keyed fixtures whose vintage drives staleness).

**Files.**
- Create: `src/store/recStaleness.ts` (the **inverted** recommendation-staleness rule + the ACA-status re-verify gate)
- Modify: `src/shared/model.ts` (the **saved-recommendation record** — the recommended strategy + its as-recommended fixture-vintage stamps + a **solver/engine-CODE version stamp** + `seedA`/`seedB`/goal — under the single Act-4 `schemaVersion`-3 bump; the **"never-depleted" surplus / serialization sentinels** decided here, not `Infinity`/`NaN` — `archive/do-not-disturb/009`. *Execution status is **not** tracked — the sentinel denotes a non-depleting surplus value, never an execution state; the inversion below is precautionary, not a claim the action was taken.*), `src/store/staleness.ts` (Act 3 — register the recommendation as a surface whose staleness rule **inverts**), `src/ui/ReEntry.tsx` (Act 3 — surface the re-solved recommendation + the "may no longer be advised" copy)
- Test: `src/store/__tests__/recStaleness.test.ts`, `src/ui/__tests__/reentry-recommendation.test.tsx`

**Approach.**
- **The staleness rule INVERTS for a recommendation (contract #8b).** Act-3's spine rule: **re-present** a saved verdict under its **saved** fixture vintage (so the number matches the screenshot), and only **re-run** under current fixtures on demand. A recommendation **cannot** do that — it is a real action a user may execute with real dollars — so the rule inverts: **a stale saved recommendation always re-solves under CURRENT fixtures** before it is shown, and is **never** re-presented under its saved vintage as if still advised. The saved record's as-recommended vintage is kept **for provenance + staleness detection**, never for re-presentation-as-current.
- **The staleness copy reads as an action warning, not a number drift.** When the saved recommendation's fixture vintage (tax-table / RMD-age rule / senior-bonus sunset / ACA-PTC regime / IRMAA brackets) has moved since save, the copy reads ***"the action we recommended may no longer be advised"*** and the re-solved current recommendation is shown alongside — **not** *"your number drifted"* (which would be true for the spine verdict but dangerously wrong for an action). A changed recommendation is surfaced as a changed *recommendation*, calmly, with its new grade. **The quietest stale shape is the *same* strategy whose outcome degraded** — the re-solve returns the same recommended strategy but its grade fell from "just do it" to "coin-flip," or its delta turned **negative** (the once-helpful action now hurts) under current law. That reads "still fine" to a user who recognizes their familiar strategy, so it carries its own copy distinct from both "may no longer be advised" and silent re-presentation — *"this is still the best of the options, but it's now a coin-flip / no longer ahead — here's what changed,"* surfacing the degraded grade or sign flip prominently.
- **A recommendation is stale when ANY rec-relevant fixture moves.** The recommendation depends on more fixtures than the spine verdict (the spine is tax-blind): a saved rec is flagged stale when the **tax-table vintage**, the **RMD-age rule**, the **senior-bonus-deduction sunset**, the **ACA-PTC legislative regime**, **or** the **IRMAA brackets** have moved (findings §Strand 5 + healthcare). Each routes through the canonical `src/engine/constants/` vintage stamps — no fixture vintage is re-typed here. **A saved rec is also stale on model-input drift, not only fixture drift:** a confirmed per-bucket **balance/split change** (a conversion sized to a specific pre-tax pool is far more balance-sensitive than the tax-blind spine — a drifted pool can trip an ACA cliff or make the saved amount infeasible) and a **budget-window advance** (a time-boxed line past its `endYear`) both flag it — reusing Act-3's existing per-bucket balance-drift confirm, not a new mechanism. And because the selected number is reproducible from `(model, seedA, seedB, goal)` **only given fixed solver code**, a **solver/engine-CODE version change is itself a staleness trigger** (grid resolution, ε, objective quantization, or tie-break order all move the winner/grade for identical fixtures): it forces a re-solve and surfaces the delta as a recommendation change, never hidden behind byte-identity.
- **Re-verify the ACA legislative status — it can flip the whole model (contract #8b; R24; healthcare §2).** The enhanced-ACA-subsidy status is **live, possibly-retroactive policy**; if the enhanced regime is restored (possibly retroactively), the **400% FPL cliff disappears** and the pre-65 conversion calculus **flips** — which can **invert which strategy wins**. `recStaleness.ts` carries the ACA-status as a first-class staleness driver: a saved recommendation computed under the 2026 cliff-on base case is **always** flagged for re-solve if the legislative status has changed since save. (The build-time re-verify of the legislative status itself is a roadmap validation gate, `verify:aca`; this unit consumes its result.) Distinguish a **forward** regime change (re-solve forward, as above) from a **retroactive** one: the enhanced regime could be restored *possibly retroactively to 2026* (healthcare §2), so a past action's cost can change after the fact — which forward-re-solve does not address. For the retroactive case surface a distinct, generic note — *"a recent law change may apply to a past year, so an action you may already have taken could now carry a different cost — worth revisiting with a pro"* — phrased generically because execution and tax-year are not tracked.
- **Gate-red-at-re-entry fallback (the undefined-state guard).** The re-solve calls Unit 15, which refuses to emit a recommendation while the oracle-cleared token is absent (contract #1). The token is withheld for **more than one reason**, and the fallback **branches on WHY** rather than always blaming the law: **(a)** the **ACA legislative status is unverified** (re-verified every build, so it can be unverified exactly when the law is in flux — the case this unit most cares about) → *"we can no longer stand behind this and cannot currently re-verify the law — do not act on it until the status is confirmed"*; **(b)** a **rec-relevant primary is still directional or ε is uncalibrated** (the pre-launch / un-pinned-build state, which contract #2 makes the *default* until pinning completes) → *"this recommendation isn't validated on this version yet — don't act on it until it is"*. In every case neither path is legal — the inversion forbids re-presenting the stale rec as advised, and the gate forbids a re-solve — so the saved strategy is shown **clearly marked** with the matching reason, never re-presented as still-advised and never silently suppressed. (`recStaleness.ts` reads the token's withheld-reason; it does not assume the law is the cause.)
- **The saved-recommendation record's "never-depleted" surplus / serialization sentinels (`archive/do-not-disturb/009`).** Decide the saved record's sentinel shape **before the schema locks**: a recommendation the user saved but a field like "expected surplus = never depleted" must serialize without `Infinity`/`NaN` silently becoming `null` through `JSON.stringify`/IndexedDB. Use an explicit out-of-range sentinel, the same discipline the spine model uses (see [docs/architecture.md §7.3 / §8](../architecture.md)). (Execution status is not tracked — the sentinel is a surplus value, not an execution flag.)
- **The v2→v3 vault upgrade — its trigger, and the first-save-mints-v3 path (the schema-ladder hand-forward, inherits Act-1 atomicity).** **The v3 bump fires on exactly one event: the first *explicit save of a recommendation*** (Unit 16's deliberate commit gesture) — never on a goal-pick, never on a solve, never on an un-saved hypothetical (so `seedB` is always derived and the goal always set before a v3 record exists; a v3 blob with a null `seedB` is unreachable). Persisting the saved-recommendation record + `seedB`/goal is the first **v3** disk write; on an **existing on-disk vault** it goes through Act-1's **model-only re-encrypt** (same DK, fresh IV, **wraps untouched**) — the same atomic path Act 3 uses for v1→v2 — so an **aborted v2→v3 upgrade still decrypts the intact v2** (a half-written vault is the same catastrophic class as Act-1's first-save-atomicity landmine). **A brand-new user whose first-ever Save *is* a saved recommendation** (intake → budget → recommendation-invite → buckets → goal → commit, with no prior v1/v2 on disk) mints a **v3 blob directly** through Act-1's keyed first-save path — the first Save writes whatever `schemaVersion` the in-memory shape implies (v1, v2, or v3); the model-only re-encrypt *upgrade* path is only for an existing on-disk vault advancing versions, never a precondition for reaching v3.
- **Re-solve reuses the saved seeds for continuity, current fixtures for honesty.** The re-solve runs Unit 15 on the **saved `seedA`/`seedB`/goal** (so the futures are continuous with what the user saw — not a fresh random scenario) but under the **current** fixtures **and the current confirmed balances/budget** (so the strategy is current) — never the saved *pre-confirm* balances; a drifted per-bucket split re-confirms through Act-3's gate **before** the re-solve. This keeps the comparison honest: the same futures, the now-current law and model — what changed is the *advice*, isolated from sampling noise. **Reusing the saved `seedB` under changed fixtures does NOT contaminate the held-out, even when the candidate set's size/shape changes** (a moved grid resolution, RMD age, or feasibility): the hold-out's validity comes from `seedB ⊥ seedA` (the seed-input decorrelation, contract #2), **not** from any binding to a particular candidate set — `seedB` is an independent *scenario* set, not a per-candidate object, so a winner **selected on A** and **graded on the saved `seedB`-family over the current candidates** is unbiased regardless of how the candidate set changed. The one invariant that must hold: the re-solve **recomputes** the grade on the `seedB`-family over the *current* candidates (it never re-presents the saved grade), and it still **selects on A, grades on B** — never selecting on B. (This is the resolution of the apparent "stale `seedB` vector" concern: continuity-of-futures = same seeds; hold-out validity = `B ⊥ A`; the two do not conflict.)

**Patterns to follow.** Act-3 staleness map + re-entry (invert the spine rule for the recommendation surface); `archive/do-not-disturb/009` (JSON-persisted sentinels — decide before the schema locks); `burned/062` (no in-range default for the saved-goal / vintage fields); findings §Strand 5 + healthcare §2 (the ACA legislative landmine).

**Test scenarios.**
- Happy path (fresh): a saved recommendation whose **fixtures AND solver/engine version** are unchanged since save re-presents with no staleness warning (re-solved under current = saved, byte-identical).
- Edge case (inverted rule): a saved recommendation with a moved tax-table vintage **re-solves under current fixtures** and reads *"the action we recommended may no longer be advised,"* **not** *"your number drifted"* — and is **never** re-presented under the saved vintage as still-advised.
- Edge case (rec-relevant fixtures): the saved rec is flagged stale when **any** of {tax-table, RMD-age rule, senior-bonus sunset, ACA-PTC regime, IRMAA brackets} has moved; an unrelated app-default change (a spine-only vintage) does **not** falsely flag the recommendation.
- Edge case (model-input drift): a saved rec whose pre-tax/Roth split drifted re-confirms the split through Act-3's gate, then re-solves on the confirmed balances; a re-solve on the stale saved split fails the suite.
- Edge case (solver-version): a saved rec re-entered under a newer solver/engine code version is flagged and re-solved even with identical fixtures.
- Edge case (same-strategy degraded): a saved rec whose strategy is unchanged but whose grade fell to "coin-flip" (or whose delta turned negative) surfaces the degraded grade / sign flip prominently, not as "no change."
- Edge case (gate red): the ACA status is unverified (or the oracle-cleared token withheld) at re-entry → the saved rec is shown un-re-verifiable ("do not act until confirmed"), not as current and not as still-advised.
- Edge case (retroactive ACA): a retroactive ACA restoration emits the distinct past-cost note, not only the forward "may no longer be advised."
- Error path (v2→v3 atomicity): an aborted v2→v3 upgrade (saving the first recommendation) still decrypts the intact v2 vault (Act-1 model-only re-encrypt, `archive/do-not-disturb/009`).
- Edge case (ACA flip): a saved rec computed under the cliff-on base case is flagged for re-solve when the ACA legislative status changed since save; the re-solved recommendation can be a **different** strategy (the ranking inverted) and is surfaced calmly with its new grade (R24).
- Edge case (seed continuity): the re-solve reuses the saved `seedA`/`seedB`/goal under current fixtures — the futures are continuous; what changed is isolated to the law, not sampling noise.
- Error path (sentinels): a saved recommendation with a "never depleted" surplus field round-trips through encrypt→persist→decrypt without the sentinel collapsing to `null` (`archive/do-not-disturb/009`).
- Integration (re-entry): a returning user with a saved recommendation sees the re-solved current recommendation + the action-warning copy through the Act-3 re-entry surface, behind the unlock — never a silently stale strategy presented as current.

**Verification.** A returning user's saved recommendation is treated as the executed action it is — re-solved under current fixtures (on the saved seeds, for continuity), flagged stale when any rec-relevant fixture or the ACA legislative status has moved, surfaced with *"the action we recommended may no longer be advised"* rather than a number-drift note, and never re-presented as still-advised under a stale vintage. **A stale strategy silently presented as current is the cardinal sin extended across time (R25); this unit is the structural guard against it.** **Passing the N=1 cold-read on Unit 17's staleness copy is an Act-4 exit condition** (the cold-read judges *tone*, Unit 14 judges *correctness*): the *"may no longer be advised"* copy (honest, not alarmist), the **same-strategy-degraded** case (a familiar strategy whose grade fell or delta turned negative must read as honest recalibration, never "still fine / no change"), the **retroactive-ACA past-cost** note (informational, not catastrophizing), and the **gate-red** reasons (each honest about why, never blaming the law when a primary is merely un-pinned) are each first-class subjects.

**Execution note.** Test-first on the **inversion** (a moved fixture must re-solve, never re-present) and the **sentinel round-trip** (decide the saved-record serialization before the schema locks) — both are correctness anchors, not UI polish.
